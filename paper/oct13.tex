\begin{abstract}
We study nearest-neighbor walks whose first departure from each site has
an inward mean prescribed by a convex gauge, and whose later departures
are simple random walk. The first-departure laws may depend on the past,
and the drift strength may have spatial defects of codimension one.
One potential argument gives an almost-sure filled limit shape, uniform
conical local times, and recurrence. In the Euclidean case it retains
the sharp planar fluctuation exponent, polylogarithmic radius bounds
in higher dimensions, and the local-time limit laws. A quadratic
martingale gives Gaussian and iterated-logarithm laws for a weighted
radial moment for every convex body. An energy identity additionally
controls the number of sites with small local time. The proof combines
exit-time stopping, contact-point positivity, and a finite-scale
calibration, and includes the three previously studied models. A squared-Green
identity makes the variance estimates local, and Green differences give
bulk oscillation lower bounds for every convex gauge. A sharp anisotropic
cone inequality also strengthens the Sobolev estimate used in the
calibration approach.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

An inward-excited walk is attracted toward a prescribed center only when
it leaves a site for the first time. After that departure the site has
no remaining bias. In the Euclidean example, the first step from
$x\ne0$ has mean $-\kappa x/|x|$, while every later step is uniform
among the $2d$ neighbors. The path therefore remembers where it has
been, even though its motion at a previously departed site is simple
random walk. Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} illustrates this rule.

Kozma asked whether this attraction produces a ball of radius of order
$n^{1/(d+1)}$ after $n$ steps \citep{Oberwolfach2007,Kozma2013}.
The three papers considered here establish shape theorems for related
inward drifts. The present aim is to give one proof that explains both
the common behavior and the different limiting shapes, while retaining
the fluctuation and sharpness results.

The local times provide the useful description of the growth. A site
deep inside the range has been visited many times, whereas a site near
the boundary has only recently supplied its inward step. The limiting
profile is a cone: its height is proportional to the distance from the
boundary measured in the appropriate gauge. Integrating a cone of
height $r$ over a body of diameter $r$ gives a mass of order $r^{d+1}$.
Since the sum of the local times is exactly $n$, this identifies the
scale $r\asymp n^{1/(d+1)}$. The potential identity below determines
both the cone and its multiplicative constant.


The range of an inward-excited walk is determined at first order by its
mean excitation. The probabilities realizing that mean, and spatial
changes confined to sufficiently thin sets, affect only lower-order
terms. We make this principle quantitative. The argument unifies
\citet{Singh2026}, \citet{BouRabeePeres2026CERW}, and
\citet{Nguyen2026}; all three models are special cases of the same
assumptions below.

\subsection{The model and its geometry}\label{sec:model}

A convex gauge records the shape toward which the drift points. Its
subgradient at $x$ is a normal to a supporting hyperplane of the level
set through $x$; it need not be parallel to $x$. For a Euclidean ball
it is $x/|x|$, for an $\ell^1$ ball one may take the coordinate signs,
and for a body with corners there can be several choices. The argument
will be uniform over those choices. This is why the model is stated
using subgradients rather than a smooth boundary.

Fix $d\ge2$. Let $K\subset\R^d$ be compact and convex, with $0$ in its
interior, and let $\rho(x)=\inf\{t>0:x\in tK\}$ be its gauge.
Write $V=|K|$ and $\omega_d=|B(0,1)|$. Symmetry and smoothness of $K$
are not required. Fix $\kappa>0$, subgradients
$\xi(x)\in\partial\rho(x)$ for $x\in\Z^d\setminus\{0\}$, and weights
$\lambda(x)\ge\lambda_->0$. Assume that, for a
constant $A<\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-defect}
 \sum_{x\in\Z^d\cap B(y,s)}|\lambda(x)-1|
 \le A s^{d-1}\qquad(y\in\R^d,\ s\ge1).
\end{equation}
Set $\xi(0)=0$ and $\lambda(0)=1$. Taking $s=1$ above gives
$\lambda(x)\le1+A$, so no separate upper bound is needed.

The walk starts at $X_0=0$. On its first departure from $x$, its
conditional step distribution, given the entire past, can be any
distribution on $\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_d\}$ with mean
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-mean}
 -\kappa\lambda(x)\xi(x).
\end{equation}
On every later departure it chooses its neighbor uniformly. The
first departure from zero may have any mean-zero nearest-neighbor law.
Only existence of the distributions in \eqref{eq:model-mean} is
assumed; in particular no lower bound on individual probabilities is
required. The fields $\lambda$ and $\xi$ are deterministic. Allowing
the realizing distributions to depend on the past is useful because
every estimate below uses their conditional means and bounded steps.

Condition~\eqref{eq:model-defect} allows the drift strength to differ
from its nominal value on a thin set. The sum in any ball grows like
its surface area, rather than its volume. Coordinate hyperplanes are
an important example: they are precisely where Nguyen's transition
rule differs from a constant multiple of the $\ell^1$ subgradient.
The condition is local in the center $y$ because the potential estimates
must hold near every possible unvisited site. The particular
probabilities realizing the mean can vary with the past; their effect
on the Green representation is a lower-order error.

\subsection{Shape, occupation, and fluctuations}\label{sec:statements}

Put
\begin{gather}
 \ell_n(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\ind_{\{X_j=x\}},\qquad
 A_n=\{x:\ell_n(x)>0\},\qquad
 D_n=\bigcup_{x\in A_n}\bigl(x+[-1/2,1/2)^d\bigr),\label{eq:range-def}\\
 r_n=\left(\frac{(d+1)n}{2d\kappa V}\right)^{1/(d+1)},\quad
 b_n=\inf_{y\notin D_n}\rho(y),\quad
 B_n=\max_{j\le n}\rho(X_j).\label{eq:radius-def}
\end{gather}
Thus $\int 2d\kappa(r_n-\rho)_+=n$. Tildes denote constant extension
of lattice functions to the unit cells. All probability estimates
below concern integers $n\ge2$. Constants may depend on
$d,K,\kappa,\lambda_-,A$ and a specified probability exponent $p$,
but are uniform over the allowed first-departure laws.

\begin{theorem}[Shape, occupation, and moment universality]\label{thm:main}
Under \eqref{eq:model-defect}--\eqref{eq:model-mean}, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item Almost surely, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently
large $n$,
\[
 \{x\in\Z^d:\rho(x)<(1-\varepsilon)r_n\}
 \subset A_n\subset
 \{x\in\Z^d:\rho(x)<(1+\varepsilon)r_n\},
\]
and $r_n^{-1}\max_x|\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r_n-\rho(x))_+|\to0$.
In particular every site is visited infinitely often.

\item Define
\[
 h_n=\begin{cases}\sqrt{r_n\log n},&d=2,\\\log n,&d\ge3,
 \end{cases}\qquad q_n=h_n/r_n.
\]
For every $p>0$, with probability at least $1-C_pn^{-p}$,
\begin{align}
 |b_n-r_n|&\le C_pr_n\sqrt{q_n},\label{eq:main-inner}\\
 B_n-r_n&\le C_pr_nq_n^{1/(d+1)}\log n,\label{eq:main-outer}\\
 \max_x|\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r_n-\rho(x))_+|
 &\le C_pr_nq_n^{1/(d+1)}.\label{eq:main-profile}
\end{align}

\item With $h_n$ as in part \textup{(ii)}, on an event of probability at least $1-C_pn^{-p}$, simultaneously
for every real $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-occupation}
 \#\{x\in A_n:\ell_n(x)\le t\}
 \le C_p(t+h_n)r_n^{d-1}.
\end{equation}

\item Let
\[
 S_n=\sum_{x\in A_n}\lambda(x)\rho(x),\qquad
 J_K=\int_K|z|^2\,dz,\qquad
 \sigma_K^2=\frac{2J_K}{\kappa(d+3)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-moment-clt}
 \frac{S_n-n/(2\kappa)}{r_n^{(d+3)/2}}
 \Longrightarrow N(0,\sigma_K^2),
\end{equation}
and, for either sign, almost surely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-moment-lil}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\pm(S_n-n/(2\kappa))}
 {r_n^{(d+3)/2}\sqrt{2\log\log n}}=\sigma_K.
\end{equation}
For $\Rmom(n)=((d+1)S_n/(dV))^{1/(d+1)}$, the same assertions
hold with $S_n-n/(2\kappa)$ replaced by $\Rmom(n)-r_n$,
the exponent $(d+3)/2$ replaced by $(3-d)/2$, and
$\sigma_K$ replaced by $\sigma_K/(dV)$.

\item For some $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$, with probability
at least $1-n^{-c}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-bulk-oscillation}
 \operatorname{osc}_{|x|\le\sqrt{r_n}}
       \bigl(\ell_n(x)+2d\kappa\rho(x)\bigr)
 \ge c\begin{cases}\sqrt{r_n}\log n,&d=2,\\
                    \sqrt{r_n\log n},&d\ge3.
       \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The oscillation is the maximum minus the minimum over lattice sites.
The same orders therefore lower-bound the maximum absolute deviation
from the deterministic cone in this window.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The weights in the moment statistic are essential: condition
\eqref{eq:model-defect} allows an $O(r_n^d)$ change in
$\sum_{A_n}\rho(x)$, which can exceed its Gaussian normalization.
They disappear when $\lambda\equiv1$.

The filled inclusion in part \textup{(i)} is stronger than convergence
of the outer boundary: it rules out holes throughout every smaller
dilate of $K$. The uniform local-time statement then implies recurrence,
since the cone tends to infinity at each fixed site. The occupation
bound quantifies a complementary fact: few departed sites can have
very small local time. The moment statistic in part \textup{(iv)} is
chosen so that its drift is compensated by the squared distance of
the walk; this is why its fluctuations have an especially direct proof.
Part \textup{(v)} shows that the bulk local times fluctuate on the same
orders for every convex gauge, including gauges with corners. The
occupation bound uses the smaller error $h_n$ at lightly occupied
sites, rather than the larger uniform error in the bulk.

For the Euclidean case put $\Rin=b_n$, $\Rout=B_n$, and
$W_n=\Rout-\Rin$. These definitions use cells for the inner radius
and include $X_n$ in the outer radius.

\begin{theorem}[Euclidean refinement]\label{thm:radial}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, with $K=\{|x|\le1\}$,
all the following conclusions hold for the same class of cookie laws.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item For every $p>0$, with probability at least $1-C_pn^{-p}$,
\begin{align}
 |\Rin-r_n|&\le C_ph_n,\qquad
 |D_n\mathbin\triangle B(0,r_n)|\le C_pr_n^{d-1}h_n,
 \label{eq:radial-inner}\\
 \Rout-r_n&\le C_p\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{r_n}(\log n)^{5/2},&d=2,\\
 (\log n)^{d+1},&d\ge3,
 \end{cases}\label{eq:radial-outer}\\
 \max_x|\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r_n-|x|)_+|
 &\le C_p\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{r_n}(\log n)^{3/2},&d=2,\\
 \sqrt{r_n\log n},&d\ge3.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:radial-profile}
\end{align}
For each fixed $\theta<1$, restricting the maximum to
$|x|\le\theta r_n$ improves the planar bound to
$C_{p,\theta}\sqrt{r_n}\log n$.

\item Let $G$ be the simple-random-walk Green function when $d\ge3$,
and put
\[
 a_n=\begin{cases}\sqrt{r_n\log r_n},&d=2,\\
 \sqrt{r_n},&d\ge3,
 \end{cases}\quad
 \Sigma(y,z)=\begin{cases}16\kappa/\pi,&d=2,\\
 2d\kappa(2G(y-z)-\ind_{\{y=z\}}),&d\ge3.
 \end{cases}
\]
For any finite set of fixed sites, the vector
$a_n^{-1}(\ell_n(y)-2d\kappa(r_n-|y|))$ converges in distribution
to a centered Gaussian vector with covariance $\Sigma$.
At each fixed site and for either sign, almost surely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-site-lil}
 \limsup_n\frac{\pm(\ell_n(y)-2d\kappa(r_n-|y|))}
 {a_n\sqrt{2\log\log n}}=\sqrt{\Sigma(y,y)}.
\end{equation}

\item In dimension two, for every $p>0$ there is $c_p>0$ such that
each of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-poly-lower}
 \P\{r_n-\Rin\ge c_p\sqrt{r_n\log n}\},\quad
 \P\{\Rout-r_n\ge c_p\sqrt{r_n\log n}\},\quad
 \P\{W_n\ge c_p\sqrt{r_n\log n}\}
\end{equation}
is at least $n^{-p}$ for all large $n$. Almost surely,
\begin{align}
 \limsup_n\frac{r_n-\Rin}{\sqrt{r_n\log\log n}},\quad
 \limsup_n\frac{\Rout-r_n}{\sqrt{r_n\log\log n}}
 &\ \ge\frac1{\sqrt{10\pi\kappa}},\label{eq:radial-lil-lower}\\
 \limsup_n\frac{W_n}{\sqrt{r_n\log\log n}}
 &\ge\sqrt{\frac\pi{3\kappa}}.\label{eq:radial-width-lil}
\end{align}
For every $a\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-smallball}
 \limsup_n\P\{W_n\le a\sqrt{r_n}\}
 \le1-\exp(-3\kappa a^2/\pi).
\end{equation}

\item In every dimension, for some $c>0$ and all large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-local-lower}
 \P\left\{\max_{|x|\le\sqrt{r_n}}
 |\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r_n-|x|)|\ge
 c\begin{cases}\sqrt{r_n}\log n,&d=2,\\
 \sqrt{r_n\log n},&d\ge3
 \end{cases}\right\}\ge1-n^{-c}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
The bounds in part \textup{(i)} hold eventually almost surely.
\end{theorem}

We also retain the logarithmic radial lower bounds under the additional
assumption of a uniform positive lower bound on all first-departure
probabilities; their full tail forms are
\eqref{eq:lower-log-joint}--\eqref{eq:lower-log-outer} below.
The proof yields two additional boundary estimates. Write
$R=\Rout(n)+2\sqrt d$ and $E_n=D_n\setminus B(0,\Rin(n))$.
With the same probability as in Theorem~\ref{thm:radial}, uniformly
over unit vectors $u$ and $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-caps}
 |E_n\cap B(Ru,t)|\le C_ph_n t^{d-1}.
\end{equation}
For lattice sites with $\Rin\le|y|\le R$, put $s=R-|y|+1$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-boundary}
 \ell_n(y)\le C_p\begin{cases}
 h_n+\sqrt{h_ns},&d=2,\\
 \log n+((\log n)s^{d-1})^{1/d},&d\ge3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

A separate analytic consequence, Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge-sobolev},
extends Nguyen's Sobolev cone inequality to every convex gauge and
improves its error coefficient. Its short proof uses the same rank
estimate twice; it is independent of the probabilistic estimates.

\subsection{The three models and the figures}\label{sec:specializations}
For the radial model of \citet{Singh2026}, take $K=\{|x|\le1\}$,
$\lambda\equiv1$, and $\kappa=\beta$. His prescribed-mean assumption
is exactly \eqref{eq:model-mean}. For the norm model of
\citet{BouRabeePeres2026CERW}, take $\lambda\equiv1$ and the particular
law $q_x(\pm e_i)=1/(2d)\mp\kappa\xi_i(x)/2$. Their strict parameter
restriction guarantees existence and, for the logarithmic lower
bounds, uniform ellipticity. Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} retains the
Euclidean constants and exponents, and
$J_{B(0,1)}=d\omega_d/(d+2)$ recovers their moment variances.

For \citet{Nguyen2026}, let $K=\{\|x\|_1\le1\}$,
$\xi(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)$, $\kappa=\theta/d$, and
$\lambda(x)=d/m(x)$ at $x\ne0$, where $m(x)$ is the number of its
nonzero coordinates. Use his first-departure kernel. The weights
differ from one only on the union of the coordinate hyperplanes,
which contains at most $Cs^{d-1}$ lattice points in any ball of radius
$s\ge1$. Thus \eqref{eq:model-defect} holds, including at $\theta=1$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} strengthens Hausdorff convergence in probability
to almost-sure filled inclusions and uniform local-time convergence.
Since $|K|=2^d/d!$, its radius is
$r_n=\tfrac12((d+1)!n/\theta)^{1/(d+1)}$, with the original
normalization. This verifies model containment at the transition-rule
level, including the exceptional coordinate hyperplanes.

Figures~\ref{fig:ranges} and~\ref{fig:profiles} retain the simulations
from the norm model of Bou-Rabee and Peres. They illustrate the
canonical kernel $q_x(\pm e_i)=1/(2d)\mp\kappa\xi_i(x)/2$ with
$\lambda\equiv1$, a special case of the present assumptions. The
same body controls both the support of the range and the level sets of
the occupation profile. These pictures also show why the question of
holes is distinct from the question of long, thin extensions.

\subsection{How the proof works}\label{sec:strategy}

The starting point is an explicit potential. If $D\subset\R^d$ is the
set of departed cells, define
\[
 U_D(y)=\frac{2\kappa}{\omega_d}
     \int_D\nabla\rho(v)\cdot\frac{v-y}{|v-y|^d}\,dv.
\]
Integration along rays from $y$ gives the exact identity
$U_{rK}(y)=2d\kappa(r-\rho(y))_+$. Dynkin's formula for the lattice
Green function gives a corresponding identity for the walk:
\[
 \ell_n(y)=U_{D_n}(y)-M_n^y+O(\log n),
\]
where $M^y$ is a martingale. The proof compares an unknown occupied
set with the one body whose potential is already known.

\paragraph{First obtain the scale.}
Stopping at exit from a prescribed ball, as in Singh's argument,
avoids estimating the range and the largest local time separately
before their scale is known. If the Green martingales have not reached
size $R$, the potential identity bounds every local time by $CR$.
There are at most $CR^d$ available sites, so the stopped clock is at
most $CR^{d+1}$. The resulting variance bound makes a martingale
excursion of size $R$ unlikely. This closes the stopping argument.

\paragraph{Why the walk cannot make a long thin extension.}
An upper bound on the number of occupied sites does not by itself
control the farthest point of the path. The crossing estimate uses the
inward cookie at every new site to oppose such an excursion. Testing
an exponential of the distance to $kK$, and grouping the compensator
by site, lets one inward first departure pay for the at most $L$
revisits to that site. The resulting crossing probability decays as
$\exp\{-ch/(L+1)\}$ over a distance $h$. Thus small exterior local
times imply a small exterior radius. The distance function also
handles corners of $K$ without choosing a supporting direction after
seeing the path.

\paragraph{Why an unvisited site reveals the shape.}
Let $bK$ be the largest centered gauge ball contained in $D_n$ and
let $y_0$ be a contact point adjacent to an empty cell. The cone
potential vanishes at $y_0$. More importantly, convexity gives
\[
 \nabla\rho(v)\cdot(v-y_0)\ge\rho(v)-b\ge0
 \qquad(v\in D_n\setminus bK).
\]
There is no cancellation among the excess contributions at this point.
The small local time at the neighboring empty site therefore forces
the excess potential to be small. This gives the conical occupation
profile, and the crossing estimate excludes distant extensions.
The identity $\sum_x\ell_n(x)=n$, or equivalently the quadratic
martingale used below, fixes $b$ at the deterministic radius $r_n$.

\paragraph{Why the variance estimate can be local.}
Apply Dynkin's formula to the square of the Green function as well as
to the Green function itself. The discrete product rule expresses its
edge energy in terms of the local time at the pole and a single
first-departure correction per occupied site. A martingale inequality
then bounds the Green noise by the local time at that same site.
In particular the potential is almost nonnegative everywhere and is
small at every empty site. This avoids estimating a spatial convolution
of the unknown local times, and gives the smaller error needed by both
the contact argument and the occupation-layer estimate.

\paragraph{Where the Euclidean improvement enters.}
For a Euclidean ball, the contact kernel has the stronger lower bound
$c|v-y_0|^{2-d}/r_n$. It controls excess volume more efficiently than
convexity alone. To bound the outer radius we look at an empty point
beyond the enclosing sphere: nearby excess points contribute
negatively there, while curvature controls the positive contribution.
The positive curvature term itself is controlled by one backward radial
integration of the almost-nonnegative potential. This bounds the
amount of excess in a cap and hence the local times near the boundary. Figure~\ref{fig:contact} shows the two observation
points. The same geometric calculation serves every dimension.

\paragraph{What the energy identity adds.}
Nguyen's calibration mechanism has a direct finite-scale form.
The potential solves
$\operatorname{div}(\nabla U_D+2d\kappa\mathbf1_D\nabla\rho)=0$.
Testing this equation against a truncated potential and a cutoff of
$\rho$ bounds a nonnegative square. With zero error the square forces
the cone exactly; with a small error it bounds the portion of $D$ with
small occupation. This estimate supplies an additional quantitative
conclusion. The filled shape and sharp boundary bounds still use the
contact and crossing arguments just described.

\paragraph{Why the limiting variances are universal.}
Once the cone is known, each typical interior site has order $r_n$
departures, and only the first has a nonuniform law. Later departures
therefore determine the leading quadratic variations. The quadratic
martingale controls the weighted radial moment; Green martingales
control local times. Their brackets are sums against the cone, so the
martingale central limit theorem and iterated-logarithm law give the
limit laws. Radius and local-time lower bounds require one additional
ingredient: exponential martingales detect deviations larger than the
central-limit scale. One covariance identity handles all these martingales, with only one
error per first departure. The vector martingale controls planar width,
and separated Green differences detect a large local-time fluctuation
for every gauge. The latter lower bounds need only the coarse filled
shape, while deterministic fixed-site centering needs the inner-volume
estimate and qualitative outer convergence. Neither uses the final
sharp outer-radius estimate.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\citet{Kozma2013} reported recurrence of the walk excited to the center, citing unpublished work from 2006. \citet*{DemboHuangSidoravicius2014} described the manuscript of~\citet{Kozma2006CentrallyExcited} for a related walk in $d\geq3$, with drift~$-\kappa x/\|x\|_1$ at the first visit to a site having an unvisited neighbor; \citet*{DemboHuangSidoravicius2014Growing} cited it for recurrence in every dimension. We take the name centrally excited random walk from its title. Its direction-dependent drift magnitude differs from the constant Euclidean drift in the radial special case. \citet*{BenjaminiDuminilCopinKozmaLucas2020} also recorded the predicted radius~$t^{\frac{1}{(d+1)}}$.

For the excited random walk with first-visit drift in a fixed direction, \citet*{BenjaminiWilson2003} proved transience in $d\geq2$ and positive lower speed in $d\geq4$. \citet{Kozma2003Speed,Kozma2005Speed} obtained positive lower speed in $d=3$ and $d=2$, and \citet{BerardRamirez2007} proved laws of large numbers and central limit theorems. \citet*{MenshikovPopovRamirezVachkovskaia2012} extended positive-speed results to uniformly elliptic walks with bounded jumps and uniformly positive first-visit drift in a fixed direction and zero drift at later visits. \citet{Zerner2005,Zerner2006} gave recurrence/transience criteria on~$\Z$ and strips for walks with multiple cookies. \citet*{AmirBenjaminiKozma2008} proved recurrence for a walk in the upper half of~$\Z^3$ that steps down at new sites. See~\citet{KosyginaZerner2013} for a survey.

The radius~$n^{\frac{1}{(d+1)}}$ and range size~$n^{\frac{d}{(d+1)}}$ are also predicted for once-reinforced walks with strong reinforcement~\citep{Beffara2011,ElboimKozma2026}, and supported by simulations~\citep{FosterGrassbergerPaczuski2009}; related predictions for self-attracting walks appear in~\citet{OrdemannBerkolaikoHavlinBunde2000,OrdemannEtAl2001}. For once-reinforced random walk on~$\Z^d$, $d\geq2$, \citet{BouRabeePeres2026ORRW} proved the lower bound~$cn^{\frac{d}{(d+1)}}$ on the expected range for every reinforcement parameter. The matching upper bound and limit shape remain conjectural. See~\citet{Pemantle2007} for reinforced processes.

\citet{DemboGroismanHuangSidoravicius2021} proved an averaging principle and shape theorem for growth models motivated by origin-excited and once-reinforced walks. Their simulations with inward first steps along a coordinate of largest absolute value or along all coordinates produced squares and diamonds. The gauge theorem proves these shapes for first-departure drifts opposite to subgradients of~$\|\cdot\|_\infty$ and~$\|\cdot\|_1$. For related continuum growth models, \citet*{DemboYang2025Flow,DemboYang2025KPZ} obtained limits described by geometric flow and regularized Kardar--Parisi--Zhang equations.

Internal diffusion-limited aggregation~\citep{LawlerBramsonGriffeath1992} and rotor-router aggregation~\citep{LevinePeres2009} have Euclidean ball limit shapes. The former has radius fluctuations of order at most $\log r$ in the plane and $(\log r)^{\frac12}$ in higher dimensions~\citep{JerisonLevineSheffield2012,JerisonLevineSheffield2013,AsselahGaudilliere2013,AsselahGaudilliere2013Polynomial}, and its rescaled fluctuations approach a variant of the Gaussian free field~\citep{JerisonLevineSheffield2014}. For the centrally excited walk, Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} gives much larger planar radius and width fluctuations; the fixed-site covariance in Theorem~\ref{thm:radial} is again expressed through the Green function. See~\citet*{LevinePeres2017} for aggregation models.

For Eulerian walkers~\citep{PriezzhevDharDharKrishnamurthy1996}, simulations suggested a circular planar range of radius of order~$t^{\frac13}$~\citep{KapriDhar2009}. \citet*{FlorescuLevinePeres2016} proved that every simple rotor walk on~$\Z^d$ visits at least $cn^{\frac{d}{(d+1)}}$ sites in $n$~steps. For clockwise planar rotors with independent uniform initial states, \citet*{BouRabeePeres2026} proved convergence of the rescaled range to a deterministic compact convex set; whether it is a disk remains open.



\subsection{Notation and conventions}\label{sec:notation}

The symbol $|\cdot|$ denotes Euclidean norm for vectors, cardinality
for lattice sets, and Lebesgue measure for subsets of $\R^d$.
We use $B(y,s)$ for the open Euclidean ball, $t_+=\max(t,0)$,
and $D\mathbin\triangle E$ for symmetric difference. The filtration
$\mathcal F_j$ contains the history through time $j$, and
$I_j=\mathbf1_{\{X_j\notin A_j\}}$ records a first departure.
A bracket $\langle M,N\rangle$ is predictable covariation;
$\langle M\rangle=\langle M,M\rangle$. The notation $M^f$ means
the Dynkin martingale for the test function $f$ and is linear in $f$.

The unit cell at $x$ is $C_x=x+[-1/2,1/2)^d$. A tilde denotes
constant extension from lattice sites to their cells. Constants $c,C$
may change from line to line, with the dependencies stated after
\eqref{eq:radius-def}; a subscript records additional dependencies.
We write $a_n\asymp b_n$ when their ratio is bounded above and below
by positive constants. Enlarging a constant extends an estimate valid
for all sufficiently large $n$ to every integer $n\ge2$.

\section{A common potential argument}

We use the assumptions and notation of Section~1. Put
$c=\min_{|u|=1}\rho(u)>0$, $\Lambda=\max_{|u|=1}\rho(u)$,
and $C_x=x+[-1/2,1/2)^d$. Write $q_x$ for the conditional law
used at the first departure from $x$; this law can be random,
but its prescribed mean is fixed. Every estimate below is pathwise
uniform over its possible values. Set $\Delta=P-I$ for the
simple-random-walk generator and
$\bar\nabla_i f(x)=(f(x+e_i)-f(x-e_i))/2$.
For a bounded measurable $D$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-potential}
 U_D(y)=\frac{2\kappa}{\omega_d}
 \int_D\nabla\rho(v)\cdot\frac{v-y}{|v-y|^d}\,dv.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Cone, layers, and contact]\label{lem:core-geometry}
For $b\geq0$,
$U_{bK}(y)=2d\kappa(b-\rho(y))_+$.
If $E\subset\{b\leq\rho\leq b+a\}$ then
$\|U_E\|_\infty\leq2d\kappa a$; for arbitrary $E$,
$\|U_E\|_\infty\leq C|E|^{1/d}$.
Suppose $bK\subset D\subset B(0,R)$ up to null sets, $y_0\in\partial(bK)$ lies in
$\overline{D^c}$, and $E=D\setminus bK$. Put
$H=U_D(y_0)$ and $I=\int_E(\rho-b)$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-contact}
 H\geq0,\qquad I\leq CR^d H,\qquad
 \|U_E\|_\infty\leq C I^{1/(d+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a compactly supported Lipschitz $f$, polar coordinates about $y$ give
$\int\nabla f(v)\cdot(v-y)|v-y|^{-d}\,dv=-d\omega_df(y)$.
Apply this to $f=(b-\rho)_+$. On each ray $y+tu$, convexity makes
$t\mapsto\rho(y+tu)$ decrease and then increase. On either part, its total
variation while $b\leq\rho\leq b+a$ is at most $a$; the two signed parts
therefore give an integral between $-a$ and $a$. Integrating over directions
proves the layer bound. Rearrangement of $|v-y|^{1-d}$ proves the volume
bound. Finally
$\nabla\rho(v)\cdot(v-y_0)\geq\rho(v)-\rho(y_0)=\rho(v)-b$
on $E$, proving the first two assertions in \eqref{eq:core-contact}.
Split $E$ at $\rho=b+a$: the layer contributes at most $Ca$ and the rest,
of volume at most $I/a$, contributes at most $C(I/a)^{1/d}$.
Take $a=I^{1/(d+1)}$; the case $I=0$ follows directly.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is the bridge from the walk to the continuum potential.
A first departure contributes its prescribed inward mean; its remaining
covariance contributes only a second difference of the Green kernel.
The only geometric approximation needed at a corner of $K$ is a
one-dimensional telescoping estimate.

\begin{lemma}[Discrete potential representation]\label{lem:core-green}
Let $g$ be the potential kernel in dimension two and minus the Green
function in dimensions at least three, normalized by
$\Delta g=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}$, where $\Delta=P-I$ for simple random walk.
For any lattice function $f$, write $M^f$ for its Dynkin martingale;
this notation is linear in $f$. Set $g_y(x)=g(x-y)$ and $M^y=M^{g_y}$.
The Green martingales have bounded increments. Whenever
$\max_{j\leq t}|X_j|\leq R$, $R\geq2$, simultaneously for all
$y\in\Z^d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-green}
 \ell_t(y)=U_{D_t}(y)-M_t^y+O(\log R),\qquad
 \langle M^y\rangle_t\leq C W_t(y),\quad
 W_t(y)=\sum_x\ell_t(x)(1+|x-y|)^{2-2d}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, for $|z-y|\leq\sqrt d$,
$|U_{D_t}(z)-U_{D_t}(y)|\leq C\log R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Standard Green estimates \citep{LawlerLimic2010} give, for a coordinate
unit vector $e$,
\begin{align*}
 \bar\nabla g(z)&=\frac2{\omega_d}\frac z{|z|^d}
                  +O((1+|z|)^{-d})\quad(z\ne0),\\
 |g(z+e)-g(z)|&\leq C(1+|z|)^{1-d},\\
 |g(z+e_i)+g(z-e_i)-2g(z)|&\leq C(1+|z|)^{-d}.
\end{align*}
At the first departure from $x$, put
$s_i(x)=q_x(e_i)+q_x(-e_i)$. The exact decomposition is
\[
 (Q_x-P)g_y(x)=-\kappa\lambda(x)\xi(x)\cdot\bar\nabla g(x-y)
 +\frac12\sum_i(s_i(x)-1/d)
 [g(x+e_i-y)+g(x-e_i-y)-2g(x-y)].
\]
Thus the arbitrary first-departure covariance has coefficients bounded
by one and appears only in the second line. Dynkin's formula, grouping
first departures by site, reads
\[
 \ell_t(y)=g(X_t-y)-g(-y)-M_t^y
                  -\sum_{x\in A_t}(Q_x-P)g_y(x).
\]
The endpoint term is $O(\log R)$ uniformly in $y$: in dimension two
use $g(z)=(2/\pi)\log(1+|z|)+O(1)$, and in higher dimensions $g$ is
bounded. Summing the second differences over the occupied sites costs
$O(\log R)$. The same estimate replaces $\bar\nabla g$ by its leading
term; define that term to be zero at the origin.

The defects also cost only $O(\log R)$. Indeed, the hypothesis on
$\lambda$ and a decomposition into dyadic annuli give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-defect-potential}
 \sup_y\sum_{x\in B(0,CR)}
 |\lambda(x)-1|(1+|x-y|)^{1-d}\leq C\log R.
\end{equation}
For $|y|\leq2CR$, each annulus contributes at most a constant. For
$|y|>2CR$, the total defect mass is at most $CR^{d-1}$ and the kernel
is at most $C|y|^{1-d}$, so the bound is constant. The estimate applies
to any subset of the defect sites.

Here is an elementary substitute for a smoothness assumption on
$\rho$. Define its nonnegative lattice curvature by
\[
 \eta(x)=\sum_{i=1}^d
 [\rho(x+de_i)+\rho(x-de_i)-2\rho(x)].
\]
Then, for every choice $\xi(x)\in\partial\rho(x)$, including
$\xi(0)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-cell}
 \int_{C_x}|\nabla\rho(v)-\xi(x)|\,dv\leq C\eta(x),
 \qquad
 \sum_{x\in B(y,s)\cap\Z^d}\eta(x)\leq Cs^{d-1}\quad(s\geq1).
\end{equation}
To prove the first assertion, subtract the supporting plane and put
$h(v)=\rho(v)-\rho(x)-\xi(x)\cdot(v-x)$. This is convex,
nonnegative, and zero at $x$. For $v\in C_x$, the points $v\pm e_i$
lie in the convex hull of $x\pm de_j$, because their $\ell^1$
distance from $x$ is at most $d/2+1\leq d$. Their $h$-values are
therefore at most $\eta(x)$. At every differentiability point,
convexity on a coordinate line gives
\[
 |\partial_i h(v)|\leq h(v+e_i)+h(v-e_i)\leq2\eta(x),
\]
which proves the cell estimate. For the second assertion, sum each
coordinate second difference along the lattice lines meeting the ball.
The points on each line form an interval, and the sum telescopes to
at most $2d$ first differences at its endpoints. Each first difference
has absolute value at most $d\Lambda$, and there are
$O(s^{d-1})$ such lines. This proves the claimed bound.

Write $F_y(v)=(v-y)|v-y|^{-d}$. Cells at bounded distance from $y$
have a uniformly bounded total contribution. On the other cells,
$|F_y(v)-F_y(x)|\leq C(1+|x-y|)^{-d}$, whose sum is $O(\log R)$.
The remaining error, from replacing $\xi(x)$ by $\nabla\rho(v)$,
is at most
\[
 C\sum_{x\in B(0,CR)}\eta(x)(1+|x-y|)^{1-d}=O(\log R).
\]
This follows from \eqref{eq:core-cell} by exactly the near-pole and
far-pole annulus argument used for \eqref{eq:core-defect-potential}.
We have now obtained $U_{D_t}$ and the first identity in
\eqref{eq:core-green}, uniformly over all poles.

Finally a martingale increment is a centered nearest-neighbor
increment of $g_y$. Its conditional variance is at most
$C(1+|X_j-y|)^{2-2d}$, for every allowed conditional step law.
Summing gives the bracket bound. To obtain the cell modulus, integrate
the kernel directly within distance $2\sqrt d$ of either pole and
use $|\nabla F_y(v)|\leq C|v-y|^{-d}$ elsewhere. The same near-pole
and far-pole split gives $C\log R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exit bootstrap]\label{lem:core-bootstrap}
Put $\tau_R=\inf\{t:\rho(X_t)\geq R\}$. For every $p>0$, outside an event
of probability $CR^{-p}$, simultaneously for $t\leq\tau_R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-bootstrap}
 \max_x\ell_t(x)\leq CR,\qquad
 \tau_R\leq CR^{d+1},\qquad
 \sup_{y\in\Z^d}|M_t^y|\leq C\sqrt R\log R.
\end{equation}
Consequently
$\|\widetilde\ell_t-U_{D_t}\|_\infty\leq C\sqrt R\log R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The point of the extra stop below is to obtain a deterministic clock
bound before applying a martingale inequality. No prior estimate of
$\tau_R$, or even of its finiteness, is needed.
For $t\leq\tau_R$, every departed site lies in $B(0,C_1R)$.
The exact Dynkin identity and the Green increment bound give
\[
 \ell_t(y)\leq |M_t^y|+CR
\]
uniformly in $y$: the sum of $(1+|x-y|)^{1-d}$ over a ball of radius
$C_1R$ is at most $CR$, and the endpoint costs at most $C\log R$.
Choose $C_2$ large and set
\[
 \mathcal Y_R=\{y\in\Z^d:|y|\leq C_2R^2\},\qquad
 \gamma_R=\inf\{t:\max_{y\in\mathcal Y_R}|M_t^y|\geq R\},
 \qquad \sigma_R=\tau_R\wedge\gamma_R.
\]
At every integer $t<\sigma_R$, all occupied sites belong to
$\mathcal Y_R$ and have local time at most $CR$. Since there are at
most $CR^d$ such sites,
$t=\sum_x\ell_t(x)\leq CR^{d+1}$. Thus $\sigma_R\leq CR^{d+1}+1$
deterministically, even if $\tau_R$ was initially allowed to be infinite.
The bounded martingale overshoot and the single final departure
preserve $\max_x\ell_{\sigma_R}(x)\leq CR$. Consequently
\[
 \langle M^y\rangle_{\sigma_R}
 \leq CR\sum_{x\in B(0,C_1R)}(1+|x-y|)^{2-2d}
 \leq CR\log R.
\]
Freedman's maximal inequality \citep[Theorem~1.6]{Freedman1975},
applied to the stopped martingales, and a union bound over
$|\mathcal Y_R|=O(R^{2d})$ poles show that
\[
 \max_{y\in\mathcal Y_R}\max_{t\leq\sigma_R}|M_t^y|
 \leq C_p\sqrt R\log R
\]
except with probability $CR^{-p}$. For sufficiently large $R$ this
is less than $R$. Hence $\gamma_R\leq\tau_R$ is impossible on that
event, and $\sigma_R=\tau_R$ gives both the clock and the local-time
bounds. Bounded values of $R$ are absorbed by increasing the constants.

There is no infinite union of exceptional events hidden in the stated
supremum. For $|y|>C_2R^2$, the pole is unvisited before exit and the
exact Dynkin identity gives, pathwise for $t\leq\tau_R$,
\[
 |M_t^y|\leq C(R+|A_t|)|y|^{1-d}
             \leq CR^d|y|^{1-d}\leq C.
\]
Here the endpoint uses the Green increment bound along a lattice path
of length $O(R)$, and each first departure contributes at most
$C|y|^{1-d}$. This proves the assertion for every pole.
The representation and cell modulus complete the proof.
\end{proof}

The next estimate converts small exterior local times into a spatial
bound. We use the distance to the whole convex body, so the argument
needs no choice or discretization of supporting directions.

\begin{lemma}[Distance crossing]\label{lem:core-crossing}
For every $p>0$ and integer $N\geq2$, outside an event of probability
$CN^{-p}$, simultaneously for $t\leq N$ and every real $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-crossing}
 \max_{j\leq t}\operatorname{dist}(X_j,kK)
 \leq C\left(1+\max_{\rho(x)\geq k}\ell_t(x)\right)\log N.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $k\geq0$ and write $D(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,kK)$.
If $D(x)>2$, let $\pi(x)$ be the Euclidean projection onto $kK$
and $\nu(x)=(x-\pi(x))/D(x)$. For any nearest-neighbor step $e$,
\[
 D(x+e)\leq|x+e-\pi(x)|
          \leq D(x)+\nu(x)\cdot e+C/D(x).
\]
Moreover, the subgradient inequality and the competitor
$kx/\rho(x)\in kK$ imply
\[
 \xi(x)\cdot\nu(x)
 \geq\frac{\rho(x)-k}{D(x)}\geq c.
\]
For $f(x)=\exp(\theta D(x))$, $0<\theta\leq1$, use the preceding
distance bound and $|D(x+e)-D(x)|\leq1$. If $I_j$ indicates a first
departure, then, whenever $D(X_j)>2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-distance-drift}
 \E[f(X_{j+1})-f(X_j)\mid\mathcal F_j]
 \leq\theta f(X_j)
 \left[C\left(\theta+\frac1{D(X_j)}\right)
                   -\kappa\lambda_-c I_j\right].
\end{equation}
This uses only the conditional mean; all second-order terms are
bounded by the unit step size.

Fix an integer $L\geq0$ and $H>0$. Stop at time $N$, at the first
arrival with $D(X_j)\geq H$, or as soon as some site with
$\rho(x)\geq k$ has local time $L+1$, whichever comes first; call this
time $T$. Every exterior site has at most $L+1$ departures before $T$.
Choose
$\theta=c_0/(L+1)$ and a fixed $A_0>2$, with $c_0$ sufficiently
small and $A_0$ sufficiently large that
\[
 C(c_0+A_0^{-1})\leq\kappa\lambda_-c.
\]
For a departed site $x$ with $D(x)>A_0(L+1)$, sum the right-hand
side of \eqref{eq:core-distance-drift} over all its departures
before $T$. Its cookie occurs exactly once, so that sum is at most
\[
 \theta f(x)\left[(L+1)C\left(\theta+D(x)^{-1}\right)
                        -\kappa\lambda_-c\right]\leq0.
\]
In the remaining tube, $f(X_j)$ and $f(X_{j+1})$ are bounded by the
fixed constant $\exp(c_0(A_0+1))$, so each compensator term is bounded
above by a fixed constant. There are at most $N$ terms. Optional
stopping of the bounded-time Dynkin martingale therefore gives
$\E f(X_T)\leq1+CN$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-exp-crossing}
 \P\left\{\begin{array}{c}
 \text{for some }t\leq N,\quad
 \max_{j\leq t}D(X_j)\geq H,\\
 \max_{\rho(x)\geq k}\ell_t(x)\leq L
 \end{array}\right\}
 \leq CN\exp\{-c_0H/(L+1)\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, on this event the first arrival at distance $H$ precedes the
local-time stop, so $f(X_T)\geq e^{\theta H}$. The argument has not
conditioned on the event whose probability is being estimated.

Apply \eqref{eq:core-exp-crossing} with
$H=C_p(L+1)\log N$, and take a union bound over the integers
$0\leq L\leq N$ and $0\leq k\leq\lceil\Lambda N\rceil+1$.
There are $O(N^2)$ choices; increasing $C_p$ makes their total failure
probability at most $CN^{-p}$. At every time $t\leq N$, substitute
$L=\max_{\rho(x)\geq k}\ell_t(x)$ to get
\eqref{eq:core-crossing} for these integer levels. Larger levels
contain the whole path, since $\rho(X_j)\leq\Lambda N$.
Finally, for real $k\geq0$, set $m=\lceil k\rceil$. Radial scaling
inside $K\subset B(0,1/c)$ gives
\[
 D_k(x)\leq D_m(x)+1/c,\qquad
 \max_{\rho(x)\geq m}\ell_t(x)
 \leq\max_{\rho(x)\geq k}\ell_t(x).
\]
The integer estimate, with the fixed additive constant absorbed,
proves the result for all real levels simultaneously.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Coarse scale, with polynomial tails]\label{prop:core-coarse}
For every $p>0$ there are $c_p,C_p>0$ such that, with probability at least
$1-C_pR^{-p}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-clock}
 c_pR^{d+1}\leq\tau_R\leq C_pR^{d+1}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, with $r_n=((d+1)n/(2d\kappa|K|))^{1/(d+1)}$, with probability
at least $1-C_p n^{-p}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-coarse}
 \begin{gathered}
 \max_{j\leq n}|X_j|\leq C_pr_n,\qquad
 c_pr_n\leq\max_x\ell_n(x)\leq C_pr_n,\qquad
 c_pr_n^d\leq|A_n|\leq C_pr_n^d,\\
 c_pr_nK\subset D_n,\qquad
 \min_{\substack{x\in\Z^d\\\rho(x)\leq c_pr_n}}\ell_n(x)
 \geq c_pr_n.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Work on the events of the preceding two lemmas with a deterministic
horizon $N=\lceil CR^{d+1}\rceil$. At time $\tau_R$, let
$b=\inf_{y\notin D_{\tau_R}}\rho(y)$, and choose a contact point
$y_0\in\partial(bK)\cap\overline{D_{\tau_R}^c}$ in the closure of an
unoccupied cell. Continuity and the cell estimate imply
$0\leq H=U_{D_{\tau_R}}(y_0)\leq\delta_R=C\sqrt R\log R$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:core-geometry} therefore gives
\[
 \|\widetilde\ell_{\tau_R}-2d\kappa(b-\rho)_+\|_\infty
 \leq\delta_R+C(R^d\delta_R)^{1/(d+1)}
 =:\varepsilon_R=o(R/\log R).
\]
Use \eqref{eq:core-crossing} with $k=b$; at sites outside $bK$ the
local times are at most $\varepsilon_R$. Since
$\rho(X_{\tau_R})\geq R$ and $\rho$ is $\Lambda$-Lipschitz,
\[
 R-b\leq C+C(\varepsilon_R+1)\log R=o(R).
\]
The cone is supported on $\{\rho<b\}\subset D_{\tau_R}$.
Integrating its approximation over $D_{\tau_R}\subset B(0,CR)$ gives
\[
 \tau_R=\frac{2d\kappa|K|}{d+1}b^{d+1}
 +O(R^d\varepsilon_R)\asymp R^{d+1},
\]
proving \eqref{eq:core-clock}. To obtain \eqref{eq:core-coarse}, choose
$R=\lceil C n^{1/(d+1)}\rceil$ so that the lower clock bound exceeds $n$,
and apply the exit event with exponent $(d+1)(p+1)$. Then $n<\tau_R$,
so the spatial and local-time upper bounds follow. For the inner bounds, also choose $R'=\lfloor c n^{1/(d+1)}\rfloor$
with $c$ sufficiently small that the upper clock bound gives
$\tau_{R'}\leq n$. The contact argument just proved gives
$b_{\tau_{R'}}\geq3R'/4$ for large $n$, and its cone approximation
has error $o(R')$. Hence every lattice site with
$\rho(x)\leq R'/2$ has local time at least $c'R'$ at $\tau_{R'}$.
These local times only increase before time $n$. The occupied cells
therefore contain $(R'/4)K$, after absorbing their fixed diameter.
Reducing $c_p$ gives both final assertions in
\eqref{eq:core-coarse}, as well as the lower bounds for maximum local
time and volume. A union bound for the two exit events retains
probability $1-C_pn^{-p}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Local noise from a squared Green function}

Fix $p>0$, write $r=r_n$ and $a=\log n$, and let $g$ be the planar
potential kernel or minus the Green function in dimensions at least three,
normalized by $\Delta g=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}$.
On the coarse event of Proposition~\ref{prop:core-coarse},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-event}
 D_n\subset B(0,Cr),\qquad |A_n|\le Cr^d,\qquad
 \max_x\ell_n(x)\le Cr.
\end{equation}
The useful noise estimate is local: when a site has little occupation,
the martingale in its Green representation has little variation. This
follows by applying Dynkin's formula a second time, to a square.

\begin{lemma}[Squared-Green estimate]\label{lem:rate-square}
There is an event of probability at least $1-Cn^{-p}$ on which
\eqref{eq:rate-event} holds and, simultaneously for all lattice sites
$|y|\le3n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-self-noise}
 |\ell_n(y)-U_{D_n}(y)|\le
 \begin{cases}
 C\bigl(\sqrt{ra}+a\sqrt{\ell_n(y)}\bigr),&d=2,\\
 C\bigl(a+\sqrt{a\ell_n(y)}\bigr),&d\ge3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, writing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-hq}
 h=\begin{cases}\sqrt{ra},&d=2,\\a,&d\ge3,\end{cases}
 \qquad q=h/r,
\end{equation}
we have, after constant extension of the local times to cells,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-one-sided}
 U_{D_n}\ge\tfrac12\widetilde\ell_n-Ch,
 \qquad |U_{D_n}|\le Ch\quad\hbox{on }D_n^c.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a lattice function $f$, let $M^f$ denote its Dynkin martingale.
Define the edge energy
\[
 e(z)=\frac1{2d}\sum_{|v|=1}(g(z+v)-g(z))^2,
 \qquad \mathcal E_n(y)=\sum_x\ell_n(x)e(x-y).
\]
Since every conditional step law is supported on the $2d$ neighbors,
$\langle M^{g_y}\rangle_n\le2d\mathcal E_n(y)$.
For any constant $c$, the exact product identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-square-identity}
 \Delta(g-c)^2=2(g(0)-c)\mathbf1_{\{0\}}+e.
\end{equation}
Let $f_y(x)=(g(x-y)-c)^2$ and $N^y=M^{f_y}$. Applying Dynkin's
formula and collecting the first-departure corrections gives
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal E_n(y)
 &=2(c-g(0))\ell_n(y)+f_y(X_n)-f_y(0)-N_n^y\\
 &\quad-\sum_{x\in A_n}\sum_{|v|=1}
       \left(q_x(v)-\frac1{2d}\right)
                       (f_y(x+v)-f_y(x)).
\end{align*}
Each site contributes to the last sum only once.

If $d\ge3$, take $c=0$. The function $g=-G$ is bounded, and the
Green gradient estimate gives
\[
 \max_{|v|=1}|g(z+v)^2-g(z)^2|
       \le C(1+|z|)^{3-2d}.
\]
The sum of these bounds over any $Cr^d$ sites is $O(\log r)$ when
$d=3$ and $O(1)$ when $d\ge4$, uniformly in the pole $y$.
The endpoint terms are bounded. Also
$\langle N^y\rangle_n\le C\mathcal E_n(y)$ and $N^y$ has bounded
increments, since $|\Delta_v(g^2)|\le C|\Delta_vg|$.
Freedman's inequality and the displayed identity therefore give
\[
 \mathcal E_n(y)\le C\ell_n(y)+Ca+C\sqrt{a\mathcal E_n(y)},
 \qquad\hbox{hence}\qquad
 \mathcal E_n(y)\le C(\ell_n(y)+a).
\]

In the plane choose a deterministic $R_0\asymp r$, sufficiently large,
and $c=(2/\pi)\log R_0$. The Green estimates imply
\[
 \max_{|v|=1}|(g(z+v)-c)^2-(g(z)-c)^2|
 \le C\frac{1+|\log((1+|z|)/R_0)|}{1+|z|}.
\]
The radial majorant decreases with $|z|$. Its sum over the $Cr^2$
lattice points nearest to the pole is at most $Cr$: in polar coordinates
the relevant integral is bounded by
$C\int_1^{Cr}(1+|\log(s/R_0)|)\,ds\le Cr$.
Thus the first-departure correction is $O(r)$ uniformly even for poles
far from the range. Equivalently, for $|y|\gg r$ its direct bound is
$Cr^2(1+\log(|y|/r))/|y|\le Cr$.
For all poles $|y|\le3n$ and times up to $n$, the endpoint terms are
$O(a^2)$, the increments of $N^y$ are $O(a)$, and
$\langle N^y\rangle_n\le Ca^2\mathcal E_n(y)$.
Consequently
\[
 \mathcal E_n(y)\le Ca\ell_n(y)+Cr+Ca^2
                         +Ca\sqrt{a\mathcal E_n(y)}
 \le\tfrac12\mathcal E_n(y)+C(a\ell_n(y)+r+a^3).
\]
Since $a^3=o(r)$, this gives
$\mathcal E_n(y)\le C(a\ell_n(y)+r)$.

The random-bracket forms of Freedman used here follow by splitting
$\mathcal E_n(y)\le Cn$ into dyadic bins. Their $O(\log n)$ cost and
a union bound over the $O(n^d)$ poles are absorbed by increasing the
constant multiplying $a$. Applying Freedman to $M^{g_y}$, whose
increments are bounded and whose bracket is at most
$2d\mathcal E_n(y)$, and then using Lemma~\ref{lem:core-green},
proves \eqref{eq:rate-self-noise}.
Young's inequality absorbs half the local time in its lower bound
for $U_{D_n}$. In the plane the additional $O(a^2)$ is absorbed by $h$.
At an empty site set $\ell_n(y)=0$. The cell modulus extends these
conclusions to the continuum; outside the pole region use the decay
of the potential. This proves \eqref{eq:rate-one-sided}.
\end{proof}

The same estimate and $\max\ell_n\le Cr$ give the familiar two-sided
uniform error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-crude}
 \|\widetilde\ell_n-U_{D_n}\|_\infty\le\delta,
 \qquad \delta=
 \begin{cases}C\sqrt r\,a,&d=2,\\C\sqrt{ra},&d\ge3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 b=\inf_{y\notin D_n}\rho(y),\quad E=D_n\setminus bK,
 \quad H=U_{D_n}(y_0),\quad I=\int_E(\rho(v)-b)\,dv,
\]
where $y_0\in\partial(bK)$ lies in the closure of an empty cell $C_z$.
Convexity gives $H=U_E(y_0)\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[Noise at the first hole]\label{lem:rate-contact}
On the preceding event, $H\le Ch$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \eqref{eq:rate-one-sided} at $y_0$, by continuity from the empty
cell. No geometric estimate of a martingale bracket is needed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Rates for every convex body.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:core-geometry} and the contact bound give
$I\le Cr^d h=Cr^{d+1}q$. Split $E$ into the layer
$b\le\rho<b+s$, whose volume is at most $Cr^{d-1}s$, and the remainder,
whose volume is at most $I/s$. With $s=r\sqrt q$ this proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-general-mass}
 |E|\le Cr^d\sqrt q,\qquad
 \|U_E\|_\infty\le Crq^{1/(d+1)}.
\end{equation}
The quadratic martingale is
\[
 Q_n=|X_n|^2-n+2\kappa\sum_{x\in A_n}\lambda(x)\rho(x).
\]
Stopping on leaving $B(0,Cr)$ and applying Azuma gives
$|Q_n|\le Cr\sqrt{na}$. The sparse-weight condition and cell quadrature
each cost $O(r^d)$ in replacing the weighted sum by $\int_{D_n}\rho$.
Since $\int_{bK}\rho=d|K|b^{d+1}/(d+1)$ and
$n=2d\kappa|K|r^{d+1}/(d+1)$, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-clock-comparison}
 \left|1-(b/r)^{d+1}\right|
 \le C\left(r^{(1-d)/2}\sqrt a+r^{-1}+|E|r^{-d}\right).
\end{equation}
The first two terms are at most $C\sqrt q$; hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-general-conclusion}
 |b-r|\le Cr\sqrt q,\qquad
 \max_x|\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r-\rho(x))_+|
 \le Crq^{1/(d+1)}.
\end{equation}
Here we used the cone identity, \eqref{eq:rate-crude} and
\eqref{eq:rate-general-mass}; their errors are bounded by the right side.
Apply the distance crossing estimate \eqref{eq:core-crossing} with
$k=r$. Outside $rK$ the local time is at most
$Crq^{1/(d+1)}$. Since $\rho$ is Lipschitz, it follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-general-outer}
 \max_{j\le n}\rho(X_j)\le r+Crq^{1/(d+1)}a.
\end{equation}
These prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(ii)}. Taking $p>1$ and
applying Borel--Cantelli gives the filled inclusions and uniform cone
law in part \textup{(i)}; at each fixed site the cone grows linearly in
$r_n$, proving recurrence.

Now let $\rho(v)=|v|$. We retain $r,a,b,E,h,q$ above, so $b=R_{\rm in}(n)$.
For $|v|\ge b=|y_0|$ the exact identity
\[
 2(|v|^2-v\cdot y_0)=|v-y_0|^2+|v|^2-b^2
\]
gives
\[
 u_v\cdot\frac{v-y_0}{|v-y_0|^d}
 \ge\frac{|v-y_0|^{2-d}}{2|v|}\ge c r^{1-d}
 \qquad(v\in E).
\]
Thus the contact estimate improves the volume and inner radius bounds to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-euclidean-inner}
 |E|\le Cr^{d-1}h=Cr^dq,\quad |b-r|\le Crq,
 \quad |D_n\mathbin\triangle B(0,r)|\le Cr^dq.
\end{equation}
The radius assertion follows from \eqref{eq:rate-clock-comparison} since
its first two terms are at most $Cq$.

Write $U_E^+$ for the radial potential with its negative integrand
replaced by zero, and put
\[
 L=1+\max_{|x|\ge b}\ell_n(x),\qquad
 w=R_{\rm out}(n)-b+2\sqrt d,\qquad R=b+w.
\]
Then $w\ge1$ and $E\subset\{b\le|v|\le R\}$.
The one-sided squared-Green bound gives directly, in every dimension,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-envelope}
 \ell_n(y)\le C\bigl((b-|y|)_++\|U_E\|_\infty+h\bigr),
 \qquad
 \ell_n(y)\le C(U_E^+(y)+h)\quad(|y|\ge b).
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Direct control of the curvature term.}
In every dimension,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-curvature}
 \sup_y\int_E|v-y|^{2-d}\,dv\le Crh.
\end{equation}
For $d=2$ this is the volume bound. For $d\ge3$, put
$F(y)=\int_E|v-y|^{2-d}/|v|\,dv$. The coarse bounds give
$b\asymp r$ and $R\asymp r$, so
$F(2Ru)\le Cr^{1-d}|E|\le Ca$ for every unit vector $u$.
Direct differentiation yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-radial-antiderivative}
 \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(t^{d-2}F(tu)\bigr)
 =\frac{\omega_d(d-2)}{2\kappa}t^{d-3}U_E(tu).
\end{equation}
For $t\ge b$ the cone vanishes, so
$U_E(tu)=U_{D_n}(tu)\ge-Ca$ by \eqref{eq:rate-one-sided}.
Integrating backward from $2R$ gives, for $b\le t\le2R$,
\[
 t^{d-2}F(tu)
 \le (2R)^{d-2}F(2Ru)+Ca\int_t^{2R}s^{d-3}\,ds
 \le Cr^{d-2}a.
\]
Thus $F\le Ca$ throughout this annulus. The same bound holds inside
$B(0,b)$ and outside $B(0,2R)$ by the maximum principle, because $F$
is harmonic there and tends to zero at infinity. Finally $|v|\le Cr$
on $E$, proving \eqref{eq:rate-curvature}.

\paragraph{One exterior estimate in all dimensions.}
For $|y|\ge R$, the Euclidean identity and \eqref{eq:rate-curvature}
give $U_E^+(y)\le Cr^{-1}\int_E|v-y|^{2-d}\,dv\le Ch$.

We now use the same exterior probe in every dimension. Given a unit
vector $u$ and $\sqrt d\le t\le r/4$, put $y_t=(R+4t)u$.
This point lies outside $D_n$, and the cone vanishes there. Therefore
\eqref{eq:rate-one-sided} gives the stronger estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-probe}
 |U_E(y_t)|=|U_{D_n}(y_t)|\le Ch.
\end{equation}
For $v\in E\cap B(Ru,t)$ we have $u_v\cdot u\ge1-t/R$ and
\[
 u_v\cdot(v-y_t)\le R-(R+4t)(1-t/R)\le-t,
 \qquad |v-y_t|\le5t,
\]
because $t\le R/2$ for large $n$. Such points contribute at most
$-ct^{1-d}$ each, whereas all positive contributions total at most
$Ch$. Hence \eqref{eq:rate-probe} implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-cap}
 |E\cap B(Ru,t)|\le Ch t^{d-1}\qquad(|u|=1,\ t\ge1).
\end{equation}
For $t>r/4$ use $|E|\le Cr^{d-1}h$;
for $1\le t<\sqrt d$ use the ball-volume bound. Thus the estimate
holds down to a fixed spatial scale, independently of the shell width.

\begin{lemma}[Cap volume controls the boundary profile]\label{lem:rate-near-far}
For every $y$ with $b\le|y|\le R$, put $s=R-|y|+1$. Then
\[
 U_E^+(y)\le C(h s^{d-1})^{1/d}+Ch.
\]
The same bound holds for $\ell_n(y)$ at lattice sites.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $t\ge s$, $B(y,t)\subset B(Ru_y,2t)$, so
\eqref{eq:rate-cap} gives $|E\cap B(y,t)|\le Ch t^{d-1}$.
The part $|v-y|\le s$ has volume at most $Ch s^{d-1}$ and
therefore potential at most $C(h s^{d-1})^{1/d}$.
For the rest, $|v|^2-|y|^2\le2Rs$ and $|v|\ge b\asymp r$ give
\[
 \left[u_v\cdot\frac{v-y}{|v-y|^d}\right]_+
 \le\frac{Cs}{|v-y|^d}+\frac{C}{r|v-y|^{d-2}}.
\]
The cap bound makes the first integral at most
$Cs\int_s^\infty h t^{d-1}t^{-d-1}\,dt\le Ch$;
\eqref{eq:rate-curvature} bounds the second by $Ch$.
The exterior local-time bound proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Since $s\le w+1\le2w$, the lemma and distance crossing with
$k=b$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-L}
 L\le C\bigl((h w^{d-1})^{1/d}+h\bigr),
 \qquad w\le CLa\le\tfrac12w+Ch a^d.
\end{equation}
Thus $w\le Ch a^d$ in every dimension. In the plane the lemma yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-planar-final}
 w\le C\sqrt r\,a^{5/2},\qquad
 \sup_{b\le|y|\le R}U_E^+(y)\le C\sqrt r\,a^{3/2}.
\end{equation}

For $d\ge3$, $h=a$, and the same inequality immediately gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-higher-final}
 w\le Ca^{d+1}.
\end{equation}
The all-scale cap estimates are already contained in \eqref{eq:rate-cap}.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rate-near-far} now also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-boundary-profile}
 \ell_n(y)\le
 \begin{cases}
 C\bigl(h+\sqrt{hs}\bigr),&d=2,\\
 C\bigl(a+(as^{d-1})^{1/d}\bigr),&d\ge3,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad b\le|y|\le R,\quad s=R-|y|+1.
\end{equation}
In particular, for $d\ge3$ the outermost radial layer of width
$C_0\log n$ has local times $O(\log n)$. If this layer reaches inside
$B(0,b)$, then $w=O(a)$. The continuum boundary bound from the lemma
and the maximum principle give $U_E\le Ca$ inside that ball.
The first inequality in \eqref{eq:rate-one-sided}, together with the
cone depth $O(a)$, gives the same local-time bound there.

\paragraph{Uniform and interior local times.}
For $d\ge3$, the layer bound and \eqref{eq:rate-higher-final} give
$\|U_E\|_\infty\le Ca^{d+1}$. Together with the cone identity,
\eqref{eq:rate-crude} and \eqref{eq:rate-euclidean-inner}, this gives
\[
 \max_x|\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r-|x|)_+|\le C\sqrt{ra}.
\]
In the plane $U_E$ is nonnegative and harmonic on $B(0,b)$, and continuous
on its closure. The maximum principle and \eqref{eq:rate-planar-final}
therefore bound it throughout that ball by $C\sqrt r\,a^{3/2}$.
On the outer annulus use \eqref{eq:rate-envelope}; beyond the range
both the local time and, except for a radial error $C|b-r|$, the target
cone vanish. Consequently the uniform planar error is
$C\sqrt r\,a^{3/2}$.

Finally, for any fixed $\theta<1$ and $|y|\le\theta r$, the excess set
is at distance at least $c_\theta r$ for large $n$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-bulk-potential}
 |U_{D_n}(y)-2d\kappa(r-|y|)|
 \le C_\theta(r^{1-d}|E|+|b-r|)\le C_\theta rq.
\end{equation}
The corresponding local-time error is at most $C_\theta\sqrt r\,a$
in the plane and $C_\theta\sqrt{ra}$ in higher dimensions.
All assertions hold simultaneously with probability $1-Cn^{-p}$ and,
by Borel--Cantelli, eventually almost surely. The all-scale cap bounds
\eqref{eq:rate-cap} and boundary profile \eqref{eq:rate-boundary-profile}
are additional quantitative conclusions of the same proof.



\section{Calibration and small occupation layers}
% ----- calibration -----
\begin{lemma}[Finite-scale calibration]\label{lem:finite-calibration}
Let $a=2d\kappa$, $b=\nabla\rho$, and let $D\subset B(0,R)$ be measurable,
$R\geq1$. Put $u=U_D$ and suppose
\[
 u\geq-\delta\quad\hbox{on }B(0,2R),\qquad
 |u|\leq\delta\quad\hbox{on }B(0,2R)\setminus D.
\]
Then $w=(u-\delta)_+$ vanishes off $D$, belongs to $H^1(\mathbb R^d)$,
and, with $A=\{u>\delta\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-calibration}
 \int_{\mathbb R^d}|\nabla w+a\mathbf1_A b|^2
 +a^2\int_{D\setminus A}|b|^2
 \leq C\delta R^{d-1}.
\end{equation}
In particular $|D\setminus A|\leq C\delta R^{d-1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The potential is continuous, tends to zero at infinity, and is harmonic
off $\overline D$. The maximum principle extends $|u|\leq\delta$ from the
annulus to the exterior of $B(0,R)$. The potential equation is
$\operatorname{div}(\nabla u+a\mathbf1_D b)=0$.
Since $\mathbf1_D b\in L^2$, the usual Fourier multiplier estimate gives
$\nabla u\in L^2$; hence the compactly supported truncation $w$ is in $H^1$.
Testing the equation with $w$, using $A\subset D$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibration-test-w}
 \int|\nabla w|^2=-a\int b\cdot\nabla w.
\end{equation}
Choose a smooth $\chi$ equal to one on $B(0,R)$, zero off $B(0,2R)$,
with $|\nabla\chi|\leq C/R$ and $|\Delta\chi|\leq C/R^2$.
Put $v=u-w$, so $|v|\leq\delta$. The equation tested with $\chi\rho$,
together with \eqref{eq:calibration-test-w}, gives the exact identity
\[
 \int|\nabla w+a\mathbf1_D b|^2
 =a\int b\cdot\nabla w+a^2\int_D|b|^2
 =-a\int\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\chi\rho)
 =a\int v\,d\Delta(\chi\rho).
\]
The convexity measure $\mu=\Delta\rho$ has
$\mu(B(0,2R))\leq CR^{d-1}$. Since
$\Delta(\chi\rho)=\chi\mu+2b\cdot\nabla\chi+\rho\Delta\chi$,
its total variation is at most $CR^{d-1}$. Integration by parts is
justified by uniform and local $H^1$ mollification of the continuous
function $v$. Bounding the right side by $C\delta R^{d-1}$ and splitting
the square over $A$ and $D\setminus A$ proves the estimate.
Finally $|b|\geq c$ almost everywhere, by Euler's identity and
$\rho(x)\geq c|x|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact rigidity]\label{cor:calibration-rigidity}
For bounded $D$, if $U_D\geq0$ and $U_D=0$ on $D^c$, then, up to null sets, $D=rK$ for
some $r\geq0$ and $U_D=2d\kappa(r-\rho)_+$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $|D|=0$, take $r=0$. Otherwise take $\delta=0$. Then $D=\{u>0\}$ up to null sets and
$\nabla u=-a\mathbf1_{\{u>0\}}\nabla\rho$.
Thus $u$ is Lipschitz and $u+a\rho$ is constant on each connected
component of its positivity set. On such a component write
$u=a(r-\rho)$. The segment from any point of the component toward the
origin cannot leave it: at a first exit its value would still be
strictly positive. Every component therefore contains the origin, so
there is only one. Its boundary lies in $\{\rho=r\}$; following each
radial segment now shows that the component is $rK$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Few sites have small local time]\label{cor:low-occupation}
On the same event, simultaneously for every $t\ge0$,
\[
 \#\{x\in A_n:\ell_n(x)\le t\}
       \le C(t+h)r^{d-1},
 \qquad
 h=\begin{cases}\sqrt{r\log n},&d=2,\\ \log n,&d\ge3.\end{cases}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The one-sided estimate gives $U_{D_n}\ge-Ch$ everywhere and
$|U_{D_n}|\le Ch$ off $D_n$. At a site with $\ell_n(x)\le t$,
\eqref{eq:rate-self-noise} and Young's inequality give
$U_{D_n}(x)\le C(t+h)$; in the plane use $(\log n)^2\le Ch$.
The same bound holds on its cell by the cell modulus. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-calibration} with $R=Cr$ and truncation threshold
$C(t+h)$. All the indicated cells belong to its discarded set
$D_n\setminus\{U_{D_n}>C(t+h)\}$, whose volume is at most
$C(t+h)r^{d-1}$. This proves the assertion without assuming a uniform
error of order $h$ at highly occupied sites.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sharp Sobolev cone inequality}
The calibration controls the defect $\nabla w+a\nabla\rho$ on the
positivity set of $w$. Nguyen's integral inequality bounds the mass of
$w$ in terms of this defect and the volume of that set. The following
form allows every convex gauge and retains the directional information
in the defect. For the $\ell^1$ ball, $a=2\theta$ and $R_K=1$, even
its Euclidean-energy version improves the error coefficient by the
factor $\sqrt{d/(d+2)}$.

\begin{proposition}[A sharp cone inequality for every gauge]
\label{prop:gauge-sobolev}
Let $K$ be as above, $V=|K|$, and let
$h_K(z)=\sup_{x\in K}x\cdot z$ be its support function. If
$w\in H^1(\mathbb R^d)$ is nonnegative and
$M=|\Omega|<\infty$, where $\Omega=\{w>0\}$, then for every $a>0$,
\begin{align}
 \int w
 &\le \frac{aV^{-1/d}}{d+1}M^{1+1/d}
 +\frac{V^{-1/d}}{\sqrt{d(d+2)}}M^{1/2+1/d}
 \bigl\|h_K(-\nabla w-a\nabla\rho)\bigr\|_{L^2(\Omega)}.
 \label{eq:gauge-sobolev}
\end{align}
The coefficients in \eqref{eq:gauge-sobolev} are sharp: equality holds for every $r>0$ and $c\ge0$
when
$w=a(r-\rho)_++\frac c2(r^2-\rho^2)_+$.
Writing $R_K=\max_{x\in K}|x|$, the last norm can be replaced by
$R_K\|\nabla w+a\nabla\rho\|_{L^2(\Omega)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume $M>0$ and put $\mu(t)=|\{w>t\}|$.
For $0<s<M$, the rank bound
$|\{x\in\Omega:\mu(w(x))>s\}|\le M-s$ gives, by layer cake,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sobolev-rank}
 \int_\Omega\mu(w)^q\le\frac{M^{q+1}}{q+1}\qquad(q>0).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the set in the rank bound is the increasing union of
$\{0<w\le t\}$ over levels with $\mu(t)>s$.
The anisotropic isoperimetric inequality and weighted coarea formula
\citep[(1.4), (2.22)]{FigalliMaggiPratelli2010} give
\[
 dV^{1/d}\int w
 \le\int_0^\infty\mu(t)^{1/d}P_K(\{w>t\})\,dt
 =\int_\Omega\mu(w)^{1/d}h_K(-\nabla w).
\]
Here $P_K$ uses the outward normal, accounting for the minus sign;
the argument therefore does not require $K=-K$.
The Sobolev and coarea statements apply since $w,\nabla w\in L^1$,
by Cauchy--Schwarz on $\Omega$ and $\nabla w=0$ almost everywhere
on its complement. Since $h_K(\nabla\rho)=1$ almost everywhere,
subadditivity gives
\[
 h_K(-\nabla w)\le a+h_K(-\nabla w-a\nabla\rho).
\]
Use \eqref{eq:sobolev-rank} with $q=1/d$ for the first term and,
after Cauchy--Schwarz, with $q=2/d$ for the second. Division by
$dV^{1/d}$ proves \eqref{eq:gauge-sobolev}.
For the displayed equality family the defect is $c\rho$ on $rK$;
the identities $\int_{rK}\rho^j=dVr^{d+j}/(d+j)$ verify equality
directly. Finally $h_K(z)\le R_K|z|$ proves the Euclidean-energy form.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One covariance identity]\label{lem:universal-bracket}
For a lattice function $f$, let
\[
 M_n^f=f(X_n)-f(0)-\sum_{j<n}\mathbb E[
 f(X_{j+1})-f(X_j)\mid\mathcal F_j].
\]
Write
\begin{align*}
 \Gamma(f,h)(x)&=\frac1{2d}\sum_{|e|=1}
 [f(x+e)-f(x)][h(x+e)-h(x)]-\Delta f(x)\Delta h(x),\\
 o_f(x)&=\max_{|e|=|e'|=1}|f(x+e)-f(x+e')|.
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-bracket}
 \langle M^f,M^h\rangle_n
 =\sum_x\ell_n(x)\Gamma(f,h)(x)+R_n(f,h),\qquad
 |R_n(f,h)|\le C\sum_{x\in A_n}o_f(x)o_h(x).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Repeated departures contribute exactly $\Gamma(f,h)(x)$. At a first
departure, covariance under the conditional cookie law differs from
covariance under the uniform law by at most $Co_f(x)o_h(x)$: subtract
one neighboring value from each variable, and bound the two product
expectations and the two products of means. Each site contributes at
most once. The argument is conditional on the past, so adaptive cookie
laws require no change.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Joint polynomial fluctuations]\label{prop:joint-polynomial}
Set $b(x)=\kappa\lambda(x)\xi(x)$ and
\[
 Z_n=X_n+\sum_{x\in A_n}b(x),\qquad
 Q_n=|X_n|^2-n+2\kappa S_n.
\]
These are martingales. With $c_K=2d\kappa V/(d+1)$ and
$m_K=\int_K z\,dz$, their joint limit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-polynomial}
 \left(\frac{Z_n}{\sqrt n},\frac{Q_n}{r_n^{(d+3)/2}}\right)
 \Longrightarrow N\!\left(0,
 \begin{pmatrix}
 I_d/d & \displaystyle\frac{4\kappa m_K}{(d+2)\sqrt{c_K}}\\[2mm]
 \displaystyle\frac{4\kappa m_K^{\mathsf T}}{(d+2)\sqrt{c_K}}
 &\displaystyle\frac{8\kappa J_K}{d+3}
 \end{pmatrix}\right).
\end{equation}
For either sign, almost surely,
\[
 \limsup_n\frac{\pm Q_n}{r_n^{(d+3)/2}\sqrt{2\log\log n}}
 =\sqrt{\frac{8\kappa J_K}{d+3}},\qquad
 \limsup_n\frac{\pm v\cdot Z_n}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}
 =\frac{|v|}{\sqrt d}\quad(v\in\mathbb R^d).
\]
For symmetric $K$ the two components of the Gaussian limit are
independent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Euler's identity gives $x\cdot b(x)=\kappa\lambda(x)\rho(x)$;
thus $Z=M^x$ and $Q=M^{|x|^2}$. Since
\[
 \Gamma(x_i,x_j)=\delta_{ij}/d,\qquad
 \Gamma(x_i,|x|^2)=2x_i/d,\qquad
 \Gamma(|x|^2,|x|^2)=4|x|^2/d,
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:universal-bracket} gives
\begin{align}
 \langle Z\rangle_n&=\frac nd I_d+O(|A_n|),\label{eq:Z-bracket}\\
 \langle Z,Q\rangle_n&=\frac2d\sum_x\ell_n(x)x
                      +O\!\left(\sum_{x\in A_n}|x|\right),
                      \label{eq:ZQ-bracket}\\
 \langle Q\rangle_n&=\frac4d\sum_x\ell_n(x)|x|^2
                      +O\!\left(\sum_{x\in A_n}|x|^2\right).
                      \label{eq:Q-bracket}
\end{align}
The neighbor oscillations are $O(1)$ and $O(|x|)$, respectively;
in particular the quadratic correction vanishes at $x=0$.
The qualitative shape theorem and cone profile turn these sums into
Riemann integrals. Homogeneity gives
\[
 \int_K(1-\rho(z))z\,dz=\frac{m_K}{d+2},\qquad
 \int_K(1-\rho(z))|z|^2\,dz=\frac{J_K}{d+3}.
\]
The covariance errors are smaller by a factor $r_n^{-1}$. Dividing
the three brackets by $n$, $\sqrt n\,r_n^{(d+3)/2}$ and
$r_n^{d+3}$ proves their almost-sure limits in
\eqref{eq:joint-polynomial}. The increment bounds
$|\Delta Z_j|\le2$ and $|\Delta Q_j|\le4|X_j|$, together with
$\max_{j\le n}|X_j|=O(r_n)$ eventually almost surely, imply
Lindeberg after stopping at a deterministic multiple of $r_n$.
The stopping has probability tending to zero by the coarse estimate.
The multivariate martingale CLT gives \eqref{eq:joint-polynomial}
\citep[Corollary~3.1]{HallHeyde1980}. The same increment bounds verify
Stout's condition, because their ratios to the respective
$\sqrt{\langle M\rangle_n/\log\log\langle M\rangle_n}$ are
$O(\sqrt{\log\log n/n})$. This gives both LILs \citep{Stout1970}.
If $K=-K$, then $m_K=0$, proving the independence assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(iv)}]
In the identity $2\kappa(S_n-n/(2\kappa))=Q_n-|X_n|^2$, the
endpoint is $O(r_n^2)$ and hence negligible on the CLT and LIL
scales for $d\ge2$. Proposition~\ref{prop:joint-polynomial} gives
both asserted laws for $S_n$. They also give
$S_n\sim n/(2\kappa)$ almost surely. The derivative of
$((d+1)s/(dV))^{1/(d+1)}$ at $s=n/(2\kappa)$ is
$1/(dVr_n^d)$, which gives the stated laws for $\Rmom(n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local times centered by the cookie potential]
\label{thm:general-local-clt}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, use the Green
kernel $g$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:core-green}.
Define the range-dependent potential and normalization by
\[
 P_n(y)=\sum_{x\in A_n}\sum_{|e|=1}
       \left(\frac1{2d}-q_x(e)\right)g(x+e-y),\qquad
 s_n=\begin{cases}\sqrt{r_n\log r_n},&d=2,\\
                         \sqrt{r_n},&d\geq3.
             \end{cases}
\]
For every finite list $y_1,\ldots,y_k$,
$((\ell_n(y_i)-P_n(y_i))/s_n)_i$ tends to a centered Gaussian
vector of covariance
\[
 \Sigma_{ij}=\begin{cases}16\kappa/\pi,&d=2,\\
 2d\kappa(2G(y_i-y_j)-\mathbf1_{\{y_i=y_j\}}),&d\geq3.
 \end{cases}
\]
Write $\Sigma(y,y)$ for the corresponding diagonal variance.
At each fixed site, for either sign, almost surely,
\[
 \limsup_n\frac{\pm(\ell_n(y)-P_n(y))}
                   {s_n\sqrt{2\log\log n}}=\sqrt{\Sigma(y,y)}.
\]
Both assertions also hold with $U_{D_n}(y)$ in place of $P_n(y)$.
A deterministic cone requires the centering estimate below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Dynkin martingale $M^y$ for $g_y(x)=g(x-y)$ satisfies
\[
 \ell_n(y)-P_n(y)=-M_n^y+g(X_n-y)-g(-y).
\]
The endpoint is $O(\log n)$ in $d=2$ and $O(1)$ in $d\geq3$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:universal-bracket} with $f=g_y$ and $h=g_z$.
The bound $|g(x+e)-g(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{1-d}$ makes the
first-cookie correction $O_{y,z}(\log r_n)$ in the plane and
$O_{y,z}(1)$ in higher dimensions. Thus
\[
 \langle M^y,M^z\rangle_n
 =\sum_x\ell_n(x)\Gamma(g_y,g_z)(x)+O_{y,z}(1+\log r_n).
\]
For $d\geq3$, summability, the cone profile and summation by parts
give $r_n^{-1}\langle M^y,M^z\rangle_n\to
2d\kappa(2G(y-z)-\mathbf1_{\{y=z\}})$.
In $d=2$, the covariance kernel is
$2/(\pi^2|x|^2)+O_{y,z}(|x|^{-3})$, whose sum over a ball of
radius $r$ is $(4/\pi)\log r+O(1)$.
The constant $4\kappa r_n$ of the cone gives
$(16\kappa/\pi)r_n\log r_n$; the term involving $\rho(x)$ and
the change from a Euclidean ball to a gauge ball cost only $O(r_n)$.
The profile error costs $o(r_n\log r_n)$. These are the bracket
limits. Bounded increments allow the multivariate martingale CLT
and the scalar LIL. The potential representation gives
$P_n(y)=U_{D_n}(y)+O(\log n)$ eventually almost surely,
which proves the assertion about continuum centering.
\end{proof}

% ----- centering -----
\begin{lemma}[Centering at a fixed site]\label{lem:local-centering}
Assume the Euclidean inner-radius and volume estimates
\eqref{eq:radial-inner}, and put
\[
 q_n=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\log n/r_n},&d=2,\\\log n/r_n,&d\ge3,
 \end{cases}\qquad
 w_n=\max\{|\Rin(n)-r_n|,|\Rout(n)-r_n|\}+2\sqrt d.
\]
For every fixed $y$, almost surely for all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-centering}
 U_{D_n}(y)-2d\kappa(r_n-|y|)-\frac{Q_n}{\omega_dr_n^d}
 =O_y(1+q_nw_n).
\end{equation}
Consequently the qualitative outer shape $w_n=o(r_n)$ already
suffices for every deterministic fixed-site limit law.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\nu=\mathbf1_{D_n}-\mathbf1_{B(0,r_n)}$. It is supported
on $\{v:||v|-r_n|\le w_n\}$, and the inner-volume estimate gives
$\|\nu\|_1\le Cr_n^dq_n$. On this annulus, eventually,
\[
 \left||v|^{1-d}-r_n^{-d}|v|\right|
 \le Cr_n^{-d}||v|-r_n|.
\]
The radial kernel at zero is $(2\kappa/\omega_d)|v|^{1-d}$.
Integration against $\nu$ therefore gives
\[
 U_{D_n}(0)-2d\kappa r_n
 =\frac{2\kappa}{\omega_dr_n^d}
 \left(\int_{D_n}|v|\,dv-\frac n{2\kappa}\right)+O(q_nw_n).
\]
Cell replacement costs $O(r_n^d)$, as does the weighted correction:
\[
 \left|S_n-\sum_{x\in A_n}|x|\right|
 \le Cr_n\sum_{|x|\le Cr_n}|\lambda(x)-1|=O(r_n^d).
\]
The parenthesis is therefore
$(Q_n-|X_n|^2)/(2\kappa)+O(r_n^d)$. Since
$|X_n|^2/r_n^d=O(1)$, this proves the claim at zero. For fixed $y$,
the difference between the kernels at $y$ and zero is
$O_y(r_n^{-d})$ on the support of $\nu$; its integral is $O_y(1)$.
The ball potential changes by exactly $-2d\kappa|y|$, proving
\eqref{eq:local-centering}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Deterministic centering in Theorem~\ref{thm:radial}\textup{(ii)}]
The qualitative shape gives $w_n=o(r_n)$, hence
$q_nw_n=o(\sqrt{r_n\log n})$ in the plane and
$q_nw_n=o(\log n)=o(\sqrt{r_n})$ in higher dimensions.
The moment LIL gives, almost surely,
\[
 Q_n/r_n^d=O\!\left(r_n^{(3-d)/2}\sqrt{\log\log n}\right).
\]
Thus both terms in \eqref{eq:local-centering} are negligible on the
fixed-site CLT scale, and therefore also on its LIL scale.
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-local-clt} proves the claimed joint Gaussian
and signed LIL laws. No rate for the outer shape is needed here.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharpness and fluctuation lower bounds}

The lower bounds use a coarse filled shape and the covariance identity,
not the sharp Euclidean radius estimates. We first record the coarse
input for every convex gauge. Write $r=r_n$ and $L=\log n$. For every
fixed $p>0$, there is an event $\Omega_n$ with
$\mathbb P(\Omega_n^c)\le C_pn^{-p}$ on which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-coarse-input}
 crK\subset D_n\subset B(0,C_0r),\qquad
 \max_x\ell_n(x)\le Cr,\qquad
 \|\widetilde\ell_n-U_{D_n}\|_\infty\le\delta,
 \quad \delta=C\sqrt r\log n.
\end{equation}
The filled inclusion and local-time bound are part of
Proposition~\ref{prop:core-coarse}; the Green-martingale estimate gives
the uniform error. Below take $p=10$.

\begin{lemma}[One common bulk height]\label{lem:lower-common-height}
On $\Omega_n$, let $a=2d\kappa$ and $v_n=U_{D_n}(0)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-common-height}
 cr\le v_n\le Cr,\qquad
 |\ell_n(x)-v_n|\le C\min\{|x|,r\}+C\delta
 \quad(x\in\mathbb Z^d).
\end{equation}
For all $y,z$ in a sufficiently small fixed multiple of $B(0,r)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bulk-increment}
 |U_{D_n}(z)-U_{D_n}(y)+a(\rho(z)-\rho(y))|
 \le C|z-y|.
\end{equation}
Also $\ell_n(x)\ge cr$ on a sufficiently small fixed multiple of
$B(0,r)$ for large $n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $D_n=bK\cup E$ with $b\ge cr$. Inside $bK$, convexity
makes the integrand of $U_E$ nonnegative, while
$U_{bK}=a(b-\rho)_+$. This gives the lower bounds for $v_n$ and,
using $\delta=o(r)$, for $\ell_n$ on a smaller ball. The
potential-volume bound gives $v_n\le Cr$. The excess set is a
distance of order $r$ from this smaller ball and has volume $O(r^d)$;
the differentiated potential kernel is $O(r^{-d})$ there. Hence
$|\nabla U_E|\le C$, proving \eqref{eq:lower-bulk-increment} and
$|U_{D_n}(x)-v_n|\le C|x|$ near zero. Outside the smaller ball,
$\min\{|x|,r\}\ge cr$, and both $\ell_n$ and $v_n$ are at most
$Cr$. This proves \eqref{eq:lower-common-height} globally.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One exponential-martingale lemma}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower-exp}
Fix $0<c_0\leq C_0$.  Let $S^1,\ldots,S^m$ be real
martingales starting at zero with increments bounded by $b$.
Suppose an event $\Omega$, of probability at least $1-\alpha$,
satisfies $c_0\leq\langle S^i\rangle_n\leq C_0$ for every $i$.
There are $c,C>0$, depending only on $c_0,C_0$, such that if
$\beta\geq C$ and $\beta b\leq c$, then
\[
 \mathbb P(\pm S_n^i\geq c\beta)
       \geq\tfrac14e^{-C\beta^2}-\alpha.
\]
If additionally
$|\langle S^i,S^j\rangle_n|\leq\delta$ on $\Omega$ for $i\ne j$
and $\beta^2\delta+\beta^3b\leq1$, then
\[
 \mathbb P\{\max_i S_n^i<c\beta\}
 \leq C\left(m^{-1}e^{C\beta^2}+\beta^2\delta+
                    \beta^3b+e^{C\beta^2}\sqrt\alpha\right).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Stop all martingales just before any bracket would exceed $2C_0$;
this changes none of them on $\Omega$.  For a martingale $S$ whose
increments are at most $b_1$ and whose bracket is bounded by $8C_0$,
put
\[
 K_S(s)=\sum_{j<n}\log\mathbb E(e^{s\Delta S_j}\mid\mathcal F_j),
 \qquad Z_S(s)=e^{sS_n-K_S(s)}.
\]
Conditional Taylor expansion, for $|s|b_1\leq1/2$, gives
\[
 K_S(s)=\tfrac12s^2\langle S\rangle_n+O(|s|^3b_1),
 \quad \mathbb E Z_S(s)=1,
 \quad \mathbb E Z_S(s)^2\leq e^{Cs^2}.
\]
For the last bound use
$Z_S(s)^2=Z_S(2s)e^{K_S(2s)-2K_S(s)}$.
Apply these formulas to $S^i$ and $S^i+S^j$, with $s=\beta$,
and abbreviate $Z_i=Z_{S^i}(\beta)$.
On $\Omega$, $Z_i\geq1/2$ implies $S_n^i\geq c\beta$,
since $K_{S^i}(\beta)\geq c_0\beta^2/4$.
Paley--Zygmund therefore gives the first assertion; use $-S^i$
for the opposite sign.
For $i\ne j$,
\[
 Z_iZ_j=Z_{S^i+S^j}(\beta)
       \exp\{\beta^2\langle S^i,S^j\rangle_n+O(\beta^3b)\}.
\]
On $\Omega$ the exponential is at most
$1+C(\beta^2\delta+\beta^3b)$; on its complement apply
Cauchy--Schwarz and the second-moment bound.  Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E Z_iZ_j\leq1+C(\beta^2\delta+\beta^3b)
                              +e^{C\beta^2}\sqrt\alpha.
\]
The average $\overline Z=m^{-1}\sum_i Z_i$ has mean one and
variance bounded by the right side of the asserted probability
estimate.  If $\overline Z\geq1/2$ on $\Omega$, some
$S_n^i\geq c\beta$.  Chebyshev, with the additional error $\alpha$,
finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Planar radii and width}

In this subsection only, let $d=2$ and $K=B(0,1)$; put
$R_-=\Rin(n)$, $R_+=\Rout(n)$, and $u_x=x/|x|$ for $x\ne0$,
with $u_0=0$. The polynomial martingales from
Proposition~\ref{prop:joint-polynomial} are
\[
 Q_n=|X_n|^2-n+2\kappa\sum_{x\in A_n}\lambda(x)|x|,
 \qquad Z_n=X_n+\kappa\sum_{x\in A_n}\lambda(x)u_x.
\]
On $\Omega_n$ the sparse-weight condition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-defect-scores}
 \left|S_n-\sum_{x\in A_n}|x|\right|\le Cr^2,
 \qquad
 \left|\sum_{x\in A_n}(\lambda(x)-1)u_x\right|\le Cr.
\end{equation}
The coarse filled ball and positivity of the excess potential imply
$\ell_n(x)\ge cr$ on a smaller ball. Equations
\eqref{eq:Z-bracket}--\eqref{eq:Q-bracket} consequently give, on
$\Omega_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-QZ-brackets}
 cr^5\le\langle Q\rangle_n\le Cr^5,
 \qquad
 \langle v\cdot Z\rangle_n=|v|^2n/2+O(|v|^2r^2).
\end{equation}
The first-cookie errors are $O(r^4)$ and $O(r^2)$, respectively.
These coarse estimates suffice for every polynomial lower tail below;
the exact almost-sure bracket limits and LIL constants were already
proved in Proposition~\ref{prop:joint-polynomial}.

The unweighted moment radius
\[
 T_n=\left(\frac3{2\pi}\sum_{x\in A_n}|x|\right)^{1/3}
\]
satisfies $R_-\leq T_n+C$ and $R_+\geq T_n-C$ by comparison
with unit cells.  The weighted moment radius differs from $T_n$
by $O(1)$ on $\Omega_n$ and eventually almost surely, by
\eqref{eq:lower-defect-scores} and the shape theorem.
The moment LIL in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-radii-lil}
 \limsup_n\frac{r-R_-}{\sqrt{r\log\log n}},\quad
 \limsup_n\frac{R_+-r}{\sqrt{r\log\log n}}
 \ \geq\frac1{\sqrt{10\pi\kappa}}\quad\hbox{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
To obtain polynomial lower tails, stop $Q$ on exiting $B_{C_0r}$
and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lower-exp} to $Q/r^{5/2}$.
Its increments are bounded by $4C_0r^{-3/2}$, and its bracket lies
between positive constants on $\Omega_n$ by
\eqref{eq:lower-QZ-brackets}.
Choose $\beta=a_p\sqrt{\log n}$, with $a_p>0$ small enough that
$Ca_p^2<\min(p,10)/2$.  Each sign then has probability exceeding
$n^{-p}$ after intersecting with $\Omega_n$ and subtracting its
failure probability once more, for large $n$.
Since $|X_n|^2$ and the defect in \eqref{eq:lower-defect-scores}
are $O(r^2)=o(r^{5/2}\sqrt{\log n})$, the identity for $Q$ and
factoring $T_n^3-r^3$ yield, with a suitable $c_p>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-radii-tails}
 \mathbb P\{r-R_-\geq c_p\sqrt{r\log n}\}\geq n^{-p},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P\{R_+-r\geq c_p\sqrt{r\log n}\}\geq n^{-p}.
\end{equation}

For the width set $V_n=\sum_{x\in A_n}u_x$.
The sum over the lattice disk $\{|x|<R_-\}$ vanishes by symmetry.
When $R_-,R_+\asymp r$, integration of the positive part of any
directional component over the remaining annulus gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-width-geometry}
 |V_n|\leq R_+^2-R_-^2+Cr,
 \qquad |Z_n|\leq\kappa(R_+^2-R_-^2)+Cr.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\int_0^{2\pi}(\cos\theta)_+\,d\theta=2$; replacing
each unit vector by its values in the corresponding cell costs
$O(r)$, as does the weighted correction.
The martingale $e_1\cdot Z$ has increments at most two.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lower-exp} to $(e_1\cdot Z)/\sqrt n$,
using its bracket bounds on $\Omega_n$ from
\eqref{eq:lower-QZ-brackets}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:lower-width-geometry} and $n=(4\pi\kappa/3)r^3$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-width-tail}
 \mathbb P\{R_+-R_-\geq c_p\sqrt{r\log n}\}\geq n^{-p}.
\end{equation}
The same bracket limit, the bounded-increment martingale LIL,
and $R_-+R_+\sim2r$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-width-lil}
 \limsup_n\frac{R_+-R_-}{\sqrt{r\log\log n}}
       \geq\sqrt{\frac\pi{3\kappa}}\quad\hbox{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
Finally the vector martingale CLT yields
$Z_n/\sqrt n\Longrightarrow N(0,I_2/2)$.
On $\{R_+-R_-\leq a\sqrt r\}$,
\eqref{eq:lower-width-geometry} gives
$|Z_n|/\sqrt n\leq\sqrt{3\kappa/\pi}\,a+o(1)$ almost surely.
Since $\mathbb P\{|N(0,I_2/2)|\leq t\}=1-e^{-t^2}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-width-small}
 \limsup_n\mathbb P\{R_+-R_-\leq a\sqrt r\}
       \leq1-e^{-3\kappa a^2/\pi}\qquad(a\geq0).
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[A joint planar small-ball bound]\label{cor:joint-smallball}
In dimension two with $K=B(0,1)$, let
$\sigma^2=1/(20\pi\kappa)$ and let $\Phi$ be the standard
Gaussian distribution function. For every $\alpha,\beta\ge0$,
\begin{align}
 &\limsup_n\mathbb P\{\Rin(n)\ge r_n-\alpha\sqrt{r_n},\
                 \Rout(n)\le r_n+\beta\sqrt{r_n}\}\notag\\
 &\hspace{8mm}\le
 [\Phi(\beta/\sigma)-\Phi(-\alpha/\sigma)]
 [1-e^{-3\kappa(\alpha+\beta)^2/\pi}].
 \label{eq:lower-joint-smallball}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:joint-polynomial} and the delta method give
independent Gaussian limits for $(\Rmom(n)-r_n)/\sqrt{r_n}$ and
$Z_n/\sqrt n$, of laws $N(0,\sigma^2)$ and $N(0,I_2/2)$.
The cell comparison above and the $O(1)$ weighted correction show
that the event in the assertion forces the first quantity into
$[-\alpha,\beta]+o(1)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:lower-width-geometry}
forces $|Z_n|/\sqrt n\le\sqrt{3\kappa/\pi}(\alpha+\beta)+o(1)$.
The Gaussian boundaries have probability zero. The joint CLT gives
the product bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The maximum of the bulk local times}

\begin{theorem}[Bulk oscillations for every convex gauge]\label{thm:bulk-oscillation}
Under the general model assumptions, there is $c>0$ such that for all
sufficiently large $n$, with probability at least $1-n^{-c}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bulk-oscillation}
 \operatorname{osc}_{|x|\le\sqrt{r_n}}
       \bigl(\ell_n(x)+2d\kappa\rho(x)\bigr)
 \ge c\begin{cases}
          \sqrt{r_n}\log n,&d=2,\\
          \sqrt{r_n\log n},&d\ge3.
       \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here the oscillation is the maximum minus the minimum over lattice sites.
In particular the same orders, with a different constant, lower-bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-bulk-max}
 \max_{|x|\le\sqrt{r_n}}
       |\ell_n(x)-2d\kappa(r_n-\rho(x))|
\end{equation}
with the same probability. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\textup{(v)} and includes the
Euclidean bulk maximum assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $a=2d\kappa$ and work on the coarse event \eqref{eq:lower-coarse-input} with failure
probability $O(n^{-10})$. Put
\[
 k=m=\lfloor r^{1/8}\rfloor,\qquad
 y_i=i\lceil r^{1/4}\rceil e_1\ (1\le i\le m),\qquad
 h=ke_1,\qquad f_i=g_{y_i+h}-g_{y_i}.
\]
The same dipoles are used in every dimension.
All poles lie in $B(0,\sqrt r)$, and in fact in $B(0,Cr^{3/8})$.
Set $\alpha_r=\log r$ in dimension two and $\alpha_r=1$ in higher
dimensions, $s_r^2=r\alpha_r$, and $S^i=-M^{f_i}/s_r$.

We claim that on the coarse event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-separated}
 c_0\le\langle S^i\rangle_n\le C_0,
 \qquad |\langle S^i,S^j\rangle_n|\le Cr^{-1/4}\quad(i\ne j).
\end{equation}
For clarity, the leading height in this calculation is the random
$v_n=U_{D_n}(0)$; a deterministic cone approximation is unnecessary.
Let $\mathcal E_{ij}=\sum_x\Gamma(f_i,f_j)(x)$.
Green-kernel estimates and $2ab\le a^2+b^2$ give
\begin{align*}
 \sum_x|\Gamma(f_i,f_j)(x)|&\le C\alpha_r,\\
 \sum_x\min\{|x|,r\}|\Gamma(f_i,f_j)(x)|
      &\le C\sqrt r\,\alpha_r.
\end{align*}
Here are the details behind these summations. In dimensions at least
three it suffices to dominate each dipole by its two monopoles, whose
squared increments are bounded by $C(1+|x-y|)^{2-2d}$. Their total mass
is bounded and their first truncated moment is $O(\log r)$ in dimension
three and $O(1)$ above it. In the plane, within distance $3k$ of a pole,
use the sum of its two reciprocal-distance bounds. Outside this region,
cancellation gives an increment bound $Ck(1+|x-y_i|)^{-2}$.
The squared sum is $O(\log r)$, and its first moment about $y_i$ is
$O(k\log r)$. Translating by $|y_i|\le Cr^{3/8}$ proves the assertions.
The finitely supported Laplacian terms obey the same bounds.

By \eqref{eq:universal-bracket} and
\eqref{eq:lower-common-height},
\[
 \left|\langle S^i,S^j\rangle_n-
              \frac{v_n\mathcal E_{ij}}{r\alpha_r}\right|
 \le C\left(\frac\delta r+r^{-1/2}+r^{-1}\right)
 \le Cr^{-1/2}\log n=o(r^{-1/4}).
\]
The first-cookie term is $O(\alpha_r)$ before normalization, by the same
increment-square estimates. Summation by parts now gives an exact formula:
if $z=y_i-y_j$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lower-dipole-energy}
 \mathcal E_{ij}
 =2\{g(z+h)+g(z-h)-2g(z)\}-2\mathbf1_{\{i=j\}}.
\end{equation}
For the diagonal this equals $4(g(h)-g(0))-2$.
In the plane it is asymptotic to $(8/\pi)\log k$, hence to
$(1/\pi)\log r$; in higher dimensions it tends to $4G(0)-2>0$.
For $i\ne j$, the supports of the two Laplacians are disjoint, and
the Green asymptotics in the second difference give
\[
 |\mathcal E_{ij}|\le C k^2|y_i-y_j|^{-d}
       \le Cr^{-(d-1)/4}.
\]
Together with $cr\le v_n\le Cr$, this proves
\eqref{eq:lower-separated}.

The increments of $S^i$ are bounded by $Cr^{-1/2}$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:lower-exp} with
$\beta=\varepsilon\sqrt{\log r}$, taking $\varepsilon>0$ sufficiently
small. Since $m\asymp r^{1/8}$ and the cross brackets are $O(r^{-1/4})$,
its failure bound is at most $Cr^{-1/16}$.
Thus for some $i$, except with this probability,
$-M_n^{f_i}\ge c s_r\sqrt{\log r}$.

Finally the Green representation and
\eqref{eq:lower-bulk-increment} give
\[
 \ell_n(y_i+h)-\ell_n(y_i)
      +a\bigl(\rho(y_i+h)-\rho(y_i)\bigr)
 =-M_n^{f_i}+O(k+\log n).
\]
The error is $o(s_r\sqrt{\log r})$. The left side is the difference
of two values whose oscillation appears in
\eqref{eq:lower-bulk-oscillation}. Absorb the polynomial failure
probability and use $r\asymp n^{1/(d+1)}$ to finish the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The window $\sqrt{r_n}$ can be replaced by $r_n^\eta$ for any
fixed $\eta>0$, with constants depending on $\eta$.
For $0<\eta\le1/2$, take $k=m=\lfloor r^{\eta/4}\rfloor$ and
pole spacing $\lceil r^{\eta/2}\rceil$. The poles are then within
$Cr^{3\eta/4}<r^\eta$, the planar diagonal energy is
$\asymp\eta\log r$, and the off-diagonal energies are
$O(r^{-(d-1)\eta/2})$. The common-height error is still
$O(r^{-1/2}\log n)$. A sufficiently small multiple of
$\sqrt{\log r}$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:lower-exp} gives the same lower
orders and a polynomial failure probability. For $\eta>1/2$ use
inclusion of windows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Logarithmic radii under ellipticity}

Return to $K=B(0,1)$, with $R_-=\Rin(n)$ and $R_+=\Rout(n)$.
For this paragraph assume additionally $q_x(e)\geq p_0>0$
for every $x,e$; later departures already have probability $1/(2d)$.
There are $c,C,h_0>0$ such that, for $h_0\leq h\leq r/8$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb P\{R_-\geq r-h,\ R_+\leq r+h\}
   &\leq e^{-cr^{d-1}e^{-Ch}},\label{eq:lower-log-joint}\\
 \mathbb P\{R_+\leq r+h\}
   &\leq e^{-cr^{d-1}e^{-Ch}}+e^{-cr^{d-3}h^2}.
       \label{eq:lower-log-outer}
\end{align}
To prove these estimates, from any $x$ with $|x|>r-2h$ take
$T=\lceil4h\sqrt d\rceil$ steps in the signed coordinate of
largest $|x_i|$.  The Euclidean distance increases at speed at least
$1/\sqrt d$, so this path exits $B_{r+h}$; its conditional
probability is at least $p_0^T$ after decreasing $p_0$ if necessary.
Among $k$ distinct arrivals at sites of any fixed
$\Lambda\subset\{|x|>r-2h\}$ by time $n$, at least
$\lfloor(k-T)/(T+1)\rfloor$ disjoint such trials can be started
and completed by time $n$.  Successive conditioning bounds their
joint failure probability by
$(1-p_0^T)^{\lfloor(k-T)/(T+1)\rfloor}$.
If $R_-\geq r-h$, the annulus
$r-2h<|x|<r-h-1$ supplies $k\geq cr^{d-1}h$ sites, proving
\eqref{eq:lower-log-joint}.

For \eqref{eq:lower-log-outer}, let
$\Lambda=\{r-2h<|x|\leq r+h\}$.
The same trials apply if $|A_n\cap\Lambda|\geq c_1r^{d-1}h$.
Otherwise, on $R_+\leq r+h$, cell comparison and the defect bound
give
\[
 \sum_{x\in A_n}\lambda_x|x|
 \leq\frac{n}{2\kappa}-c_2r^dh
                     +2r|A_n\cap\Lambda|+Cr^d.
\]
Choose $c_1$ small and then $h_0$ large.  The identity
$Q_n=|X_n|^2-n+2\kappa\sum\lambda_x|x|$
forces $Q_n\leq-cr^dh$; the endpoint $|X_n|^2$ is absorbed
by $h_0$ in $d=2$ and by large $r$ in higher dimensions.
Stop $Q$ on leaving $B_{r+h}$.  Its increments are bounded by
$4(r+h)$, so Azuma's inequality gives
$\mathbb P\{Q_n\leq-cr^dh,\ R_+\leq r+h\}
\leq e^{-cr^{d-3}h^2}$, proving the second estimate.
Taking $h=c\log n$ with $c>0$ sufficiently small and applying
Borel--Cantelli gives, almost surely for all sufficiently large $n$,
\[
 \max(r-R_-,R_+-r)>c\log n;
 \qquad R_+-r>c\log n\quad\hbox{if }d\geq3.
\]
The ellipticity assumption is used only in the prescribed-path
trials and is not needed for any preceding lower bound.
