\paragraph{Statement.} For $0<s\le r$: (i) $\frac{r-s}r\le\log r-\log s\le\frac{r-s}s$; (ii) for
$k>0$, $(r-s)r^{-k-1}\le\frac{s^{-k}-r^{-k}}k\le(r-s)s^{-k-1}$.
\paragraph{Proof.} (i) $\log r-\log s=\log(r/s)$. Use $1-1/t\le\log t\le t-1$ for $t=r/s>0$
(\texttt{Real.one\_sub\_inv\_le\_log\_of\_pos}, \texttt{Real.log\_le\_sub\_one\_of\_pos}).
(ii) If $s=r$ all three are $0$. Otherwise, by the mean value theorem for $g(t)=t^{-k}$ on
$[s,r]$ (\texttt{exists\_deriv\_eq\_slope} or \texttt{exists\_hasDerivAt\_eq\_slope}, with
$g'(t)=-kt^{-k-1}$ from \texttt{Real.hasDerivAt\_rpow\_const}), $s^{-k}-r^{-k}=k\xi^{-k-1}(r-s)$ for
some $\xi\in(s,r)$. Since $-k-1<0$, $r^{-k-1}\le\xi^{-k-1}\le s^{-k-1}$
(\texttt{Real.rpow\_le\_rpow\_of\_nonpos}).
