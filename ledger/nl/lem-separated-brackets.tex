\paragraph{Paper (verbatim).}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:separated-brackets}
Let $\gauge$ be the Euclidean norm, and let $0<\drift<\nf1d$. There exist $0<c_0(d,\drift)\leq C_0(d,\drift)$ and $C(d,\drift)<\infty$ such that, for all sufficiently large~$n$, with probability at least $1-Cn^{-10}$, simultaneously for $1\leq i\leq m$ and $1\leq j\leq m$ with $j\neq i$,
\begin{equation*}
c_0\leq\langle S^i\rangle_n\leq C_0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
|\langle S^i,S^j\rangle_n|\leq Cr_n^{-\nf14}\, .
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

r_n\coloneqq\left(\frac{(d+1)n}{2d\drift\omega_d}\right)^{\nf{1}{(d+1)}}\, ,

\textit{$g$ and $G$ (line 407)}
Let $\Delta f(x)\coloneqq(2d)^{-1}\sum_{|e|=1}(f(x+e)-f(x))$, summed over the unit vectors $e\in\Z^d$, be the discrete Laplacian. For $d=2$, let $g$ be the potential kernel of simple random walk, normalized by $g(0)=0$. For $d\geq3$, let $g\coloneqq-G$, where the Green function~$G(x)$ is the expected number of visits to~$x$ by simple random walk from the origin. In both cases $\Delta g=\ind_{\{0\}}$; for $d=2$ see \citet*[Proposition~4.4.2]{LawlerLimic2010}. By \citet[Theorems~4.3.1 and~4.4.4]{LawlerLimic2010}, as $|x|\to\infty$,
\textit{$\mathcal M^f$ (lines 422--426)}

\paragraph{Transcription.}
\emph{Conventions.} \verb!ε! is the paper's $\drift=\kappa$; \verb!hd : 2 ≤ d!, \verb!0 < ε!, \verb!ε < 1 / d! are the standing assumptions $d\ge2$, $0<\drift<1/d$. $\omega_d$ is \verb!(volume (Metric.ball 0 1)).toReal! (as \verb!CERW.unitBallVolume d!). $r_n$ is \verb!((d+1) * n / (2 * d * ε * ωd)) ^ (1/(d+1))! (eq:radius), every cast at a leaf (checked with \verb!pp.coercions.types!). The walk is \verb!CERW.IsCERW μ ε X! on an arbitrary probability space (universe $u$); \verb!X · ω! is the path $n\mapsto X_n(\omega)$. ``With probability at least $1-Cn^{-p}$'' is an upper bound on the outer measure of the exceptional set.

\emph{Author's ruling (probabilistic closure).} The paper states the bounds ``on $\Omega_n$ for all large $n$'', where $\Omega_n$ is the event of Section~norm-event with $p=10$ (line 1532), so $\P(\Omega_n^c)=O(n^{-10})$. The Lean statement is the closure: $\exists c_0,C_0,C,n_0$ such that for every walk and every $n\ge n_0$ the set of paths on which some bracket bound fails has measure at most $C\,n^{-10}$. The constants $0<c_0\le C_0$, $C>0$ and $n_0$ depend on $(d,\varepsilon)$ only and precede the probability space; \verb!∃ n₀! is existential, so no junk value for small $n$ ($\sigma_n=0$ or $m_n=0$) can make the statement easier.

\emph{Definitions (lines 1680--1687).} $e_1$ is \verb!LatticeProb.unit ⟨0, _⟩! $=$ \verb!Pi.single 0 1 : Site d!, the first coordinate vector of $\Z^d$ for the given $d\ge2$. $g$ is \verb!CERW.latticeKernel d!: the planar potential kernel $\lim_M\sum_{j<M}[P^j(0,0)-P^j(0,x)]$ ($g(0)=0$) for $d=2$, and $-G$ for $d\ge3$ (line 407). \verb!σ n = if d = 2 then √(r n * log (r n)) else √(r n)!. \verb!k n = m n = ⌊r n ^ (1/8)⌋₊! (natural floor; $k$ only matters for $d=2$). \verb!y n i = ((i * ⌈r n ^ (1/4)⌉₊ : ℕ) : ℤ) • e₁! $=i\lceil r_n^{1/4}\rceil e_1$. \verb!f n i z = if d = 2 then g (z - (y n i + (k n : ℤ) • e₁)) - g (z - y n i) else g (z - y n i)!, i.e.\ $f_i=g_{y_i+ke_1}-g_{y_i}$ for $d=2$ and $g_{y_i}$ for $d\ge3$, where $g_y=g(\cdot-y)$.

\emph{The bracket.} $S^i=-\mathcal M^{f_i}/\sigma_n$, so $\langle S^i,S^j\rangle_n=\langle\mathcal M^{f_i},\mathcal M^{f_j}\rangle_n/\sigma_n^2$; in Lean \verb!B := dynkinBracket (stepProb d ε) (f n i) (f n j) (X · ω) n / σ n ^ 2!. Here \verb!dynkinBracket! $=\sum_{j<n}\big(\sum_{e}p(e)\,\Delta f(e)\,\Delta g(e)-\mathrm{mean}_f\,\mathrm{mean}_g\big)$ is the conditional covariance of the increments of $f(X)$ and $g(X)$ given $\mathcal F_j$, computed from the step law \verb!stepProb d ε (X · ω) j!, which \verb!IsCERW! identifies with the conditional law of the next step given the whole past. Since the compensator of $\mathcal M^f$ is $\mathcal F_j$-measurable, this is the bracket of $\mathcal M^f$ and $\mathcal M^g$.

\emph{Conclusion.} For $1\le i,j\le m_n$ (\verb!i j : ℕ!, \verb!1 ≤ i!, \verb!i ≤ m n!, \verb!1 ≤ j!, \verb!j ≤ m n!): \verb!i = j → c₀ ≤ B ∧ B ≤ C₀! and \verb!i ≠ j → |B| ≤ C * r n ^ (-(1:ℝ) / 4)!, i.e.\ $|B|\le C\,r_n^{-1/4}$.
